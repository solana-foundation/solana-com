always the first signer. It does not have to be the person moving the money.

To sponsor a transaction, your app sets its own account as the fee payer. Both
parties then sign the same transaction:

- the **sender** signs to authorize moving their tokens;
- the **sponsor** signs to authorize paying the network fee.

The sender needs no SOL at all. The sponsor needs SOL, but never holds the
tokens being transferred.

account the transaction creates, such as a first-time customer's USDC token
account.

## Sponsoring in production

Signing as fee payer for anything a client sends you is risky: someone could
drain your SOL with transactions you didn't intend to pay for. Before your
service signs, check what the transaction does, limit which programs and
amounts it may touch, and rate-limit each user.

[Kora](/developers/docs/tools/kora) is a fee-payer service that does this for you. It
signs only transactions that pass the policy you configure, and it can charge
users in a token such as USDC instead of SOL.

- [Fee abstraction](/developers/docs/payments/send-payments/payment-processing/fee-abstraction):
  sponsor fees in a payment flow, then move to Kora at scale.
- [Pay fees with any token](/developers/cookbook/transactions/pay-fees-with-any-token)
  in the cookbook.
- [Fee structure](/developers/docs/core/fees/fee-structure): what the fee you are
  sponsoring is made of.
