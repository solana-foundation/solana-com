## Addresses and private keys

A keypair has two halves:

- **The public key** is the wallet's **address**. It is 32 bytes, written as a
  base58 string of 32 to 44 characters, for example
  `DxQ6RYwhfmX66AQCf3B8aL4P9EivuXnf8HQBkFZwZZFJ`. You can share it freely:
  people pay it, and anyone can look up the
  <span className="sol-chip" data-concept="account"><Tooltip headline="Accounts" tip="Everything on Solana (balances, tokens, programs, app data) lives in accounts: records at an address, each with an owner that is allowed to change it." cta="Learn more" href="/developers/docs/core/accounts">accounts</Tooltip><span className="nojs-only"> ([Accounts](/developers/docs/core/accounts): Everything on Solana (balances, tokens, programs, app data) lives in accounts: records at an address, each with an owner that is allowed to change it.)</span></span> it owns.
- **The private key** proves ownership. Whoever holds it can sign for the
  address, so it never leaves the wallet app, key file, or key management
  system that stores it.

Solana uses the Ed25519 signature scheme. The Solana CLI stores a keypair as a
JSON file containing a 64-byte array: the 32-byte secret followed by the
32-byte public key.

Not every address has a private key.

are derived from a program and some seeds, and only that program can sign for
them.

## Signatures

<SignatureShort />

A transaction lists the accounts that must approve it. The network checks every
signature before it runs any instruction, and rejects the transaction if one is
missing or invalid. The first signer is the **fee payer**, which doesn't have to
be your customer: with
<span className="sol-chip" data-concept="sponsorship"><Tooltip headline="Fee sponsorship" tip="By default, the transaction's fee payer needs a little SOL to pay its fee. A fee-payer service such as Kora lets your app pay instead, so customers only ever hold dollars." cta="Learn more" href="/developers/docs/core/fees/fee-sponsorship">fee sponsorship</Tooltip><span className="nojs-only"> ([Fee sponsorship](/developers/docs/core/fees/fee-sponsorship): By default, the transaction's fee payer needs a little SOL to pay its fee. A fee-payer service such as Kora lets your app pay instead, so customers only ever hold dollars.)</span>,</span> your
app signs as fee payer and covers the fee.

A signature is 64 bytes, usually shown as base58.

## Sign and verify a message

Wallets can also sign bytes that aren't a transaction, for example to prove a
user controls an address at sign-in. Anyone with the address can verify it.

## Wallets in apps and on servers

- **In the browser**, users keep their keys in a wallet app. Your app asks the
  wallet to sign and never sees the private key. See
  [React & Next.js](/developers/docs/frontend).
- **On a server**, your code signs with its own keypair, for example to pay fees
  for users or to send payouts. Load it from a secret store, or sign through a
  KMS or HSM with [Keychain](/developers/docs/tools/keychain). See
  [signing in production](/developers/docs/core/transactions/signing-in-production).
- **Never** put a private key in browser code, in a repository, or in logs.
