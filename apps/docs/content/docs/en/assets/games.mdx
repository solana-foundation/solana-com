A game item on Solana is a token in the player's wallet, not a row in your
database. The player owns it: they can trade it, and any other game or app can
read it and decide what it means there. Your game keeps control of what you
define, such as who may mint new items and what metadata they carry.

The concepts behind this are
<span className="sol-chip" data-concept="mint"><Tooltip headline="Mints & authorities" tip="A mint account defines a token: its supply, its decimals, who may create more (mint authority), and who may freeze holders (freeze authority)." cta="Learn more" href="/developers/docs/tokens/basics/create-mint">mints</Tooltip><span className="nojs-only"> ([Mints & authorities](/developers/docs/tokens/basics/create-mint): A mint account defines a token: its supply, its decimals, who may create more (mint authority), and who may freeze holders (freeze authority).)</span>,</span> which define each
item or collection, and
<span className="sol-chip" data-concept="token-account"><Tooltip headline="Token accounts" tip="Wallets don't hold tokens directly. Each token (USDC, or your own asset) gets a small account owned by the wallet, and transfers move between these accounts." cta="Learn more" href="/developers/docs/tokens/basics/create-token-account">token accounts</Tooltip><span className="nojs-only"> ([Token accounts](/developers/docs/tokens/basics/create-token-account): Wallets don't hold tokens directly. Each token (USDC, or your own asset) gets a small account owned by the wallet, and transfers move between these accounts.)</span>,</span>
which record who holds it.
