On mainnet the code is the same; only `RPC_URL` and `RPC_SUBSCRIPTIONS_URL`
change. What changes is who holds the keys and how you run the asset. Work
through this list before you issue to real investors.

<Steps>
<Step title="Decide whether balances must be confidential">
Confidential Balances can only be turned on when you create the mint, so decide
before you issue on mainnet. It hides balances and transfer amounts (addresses
stay public), and an auditor key lets you or a regulator decrypt transfer
amounts. The permanent delegate can't move encrypted balances. See
[Enable confidentiality](/developers/docs/assets/issue-asset/local#enable-confidentiality-optional).
</Step>
<Step title="Move every authority to a multisig">
In the example, one issuer key holds every role. On mainnet, no single laptop
key should be able to issue, approve, pause, or move investors' tokens. The asset
has six roles:

- **Mint authority**: issues more of the asset.
- **Freeze authority**: approves (thaws) and freezes holders. It can also change
  the default account state.
- **Pause authority**: pauses and resumes every transfer, mint, and burn.
- **Permanent delegate**: moves or burns tokens in any account.
- **Metadata pointer authority** and **metadata update authority**: change where
  the metadata lives and what it says.

If you enabled confidentiality, a seventh role, the **confidential transfer
authority**, approves holders for confidential use and can change the auditor
key.

Give each role to an M-of-N multisig. For the mint, freeze, pause, permanent
delegate, and metadata pointer authorities, you can use a Token-2022 multisig
account (`spl-token --program-2022 create-multisig`, see
[Multisig signing](/developers/docs/references/spl-token-cli#multisig-signing)) or a
multisig such as [Squads](https://squads.so/protocol). The metadata update and
confidential transfer authorities need a direct signature, which a Token-2022
multisig account can't give, so use a multisig such as Squads for them.
Reassign roles with the Token-2022 `SetAuthority` instruction
(`getSetAuthorityInstruction`), and the metadata update authority with
`getUpdateTokenMetadataUpdateAuthorityInstruction`. Consider separate
multisigs for day-to-day approvals and for pausing or recovery.
</Step>
<Step title="Approve holders from KYC attestations">
Approving each holder by hand doesn't scale. Issue a verifiable KYC claim for
each investor with [Solana Attestations](/developers/docs/tools/attestations), and approve
accounts from those claims, in your own service or onchain with
[Token ACL](/developers/docs/tokenization/token-acl). To revoke an approval, freeze the
account again.
</Step>
<Step title="Write the pause and recovery runbook">
Pausing stops every transfer, mint, and burn of the asset, for every holder at
once. Write down before launch:

- who can pause and resume, and how many multisig signers that takes;
- which events trigger a pause, such as a key compromise, a corporate action, or a
  regulator's order;
- when you use the permanent delegate instead, to recover tokens from one
  account (lost keys, a court order) without stopping everyone else;
- how and where you tell holders, and how you confirm the pause landed.

Rehearse the runbook on devnet with the `pause`, `resume`, and `clawBack`
functions from the previous step.
</Step>
<Step title="Publish metadata and verify the token">
Host the metadata JSON at the mint's URI and keep it current, so wallets and
explorers show the right name, symbol, and issuer. See
[How to verify a token](/developers/docs/tokens/how-to-verify-a-token).
</Step>
<Step title="Get legal sign-off on the structure">
A token doesn't replace the issuer's or transfer agent's legal obligations.
Confirm the structure, the holder rules, and the use of the permanent delegate
with counsel before you issue.
</Step>
</Steps>
