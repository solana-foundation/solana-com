An investor sells 4 LSF, shares of the restricted demo asset from Issue an
asset, to another approved investor at \$1.25 each, paid in USDC. A settlement
agent, such as the venue that matched the trade, records the terms and is the
only one who can settle. Press **Run** to step through this tutorial's code on
a local test network (Surfpool). Nothing is sent from your browser, and you
need no code or wallet.

<Demo />

The agent sent the same `SettleDvp` instruction twice. The first time the cash
leg was missing, so nothing moved. The second time both escrows held their
agreed amounts, and the LSF and the USDC changed hands in one transaction.

## Where the price came from

The first line of the run is the USDC/USD price, read from Pyth's price feed
account on mainnet. The shares cost \$5.00 for 4 LSF, and the buyer pays in
USDC, so the code divides the dollar amount by the USDC/USD price and rounds up
to the next USDC base unit. First it checks that the account belongs to Pyth's
receiver program, holds the USDC/USD feed, and has a recent, precise enough
price.

The price is fixed into the trade record when the trade is created. The DvP
program never reads the oracle: at settlement it only checks that each escrow
holds the agreed amount.

## Why the first settle failed

```text title="Failed settle (excerpt of the output)"
Settle with the cash leg unfunded (skipPreflight): failed onchain
  err: {"InstructionError":[4,{"Custom":4}]}
  DvP error 4: DvP leg is not funded with the expected amount
  fee paid anyway: 5000 lamports
Nothing moved: asset escrow 4 LSF | cash escrow 0 USDC | buyer 0 LSF | seller 0 USDC
```

How to read it:

- The settle transaction has five instructions. The first four create the token
  accounts that receive each side's proceeds, if they don't exist yet.
  Instruction 4, counting from 0, is `SettleDvp`.
- `{"Custom":4}` is the DvP program's error 4, `LegNotFunded`: an escrow holds
  less than its agreed amount. The buyer hadn't sent the USDC yet.
- Transactions are all-or-nothing, so none of the five instructions took
  effect. The LSF stayed in its escrow, and the buyer and the seller received
  nothing.
- It was sent with `skipPreflight`, so it landed as a failed transaction and the
  agent still paid the 5,000-lamport fee. Sent normally, the RPC node's
  simulation would reject it before it lands.

The trade stayed open, so the buyer could still pay. Once the cash escrow held
the agreed USDC, the same instruction settled both legs and closed the trade
record and both escrow accounts.

## Why the issuer approved the escrow

LSF token accounts start frozen until the issuer approves them, and the trade's
escrow is a new LSF token account owned by the trade record. So before the
seller could fund the asset leg, the issuer approved (thawed) the escrow, as it
would a new investor. The asset's rules apply to the trade too: the issuer
could pause the asset or freeze the escrow while the trade is open (see
[supported tokens](/developers/docs/defi/dvp-program#supported-tokens)).
