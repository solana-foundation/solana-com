As the issuer, you can apply controls that the network enforces. In this step
you use the simplest: freeze the holder's account, watch their transfer fail,
then thaw it. Step 3 made your wallet the asset's freeze authority, so you can.

## Freeze and thaw the holder

Save this as `freeze-holder.ts` next to `client.ts` and run
`npx tsx freeze-holder.ts`. It reads the asset from `asset.json` (`exf.json` in
the **CLI** tab) and the holder from `holder.json`.

<Freeze />

What the code does:

- `findAssociatedTokenPda(...)` works out the holder's EXF token account, the
  one step 4 opened.
- `sendBack()` builds a transfer of 10 EXF from the holder back to you, signed
  by the holder. Your wallet pays the fee.

Then, in order:

1. **Freeze.** `freezeAccount(...)`, signed by your wallet as the freeze
   authority, freezes the holder's EXF account.
2. **Try a transfer.** Token-2022 rejects any transfer out of a frozen account
   (error 17, `AccountFrozen`). It fails in the RPC node's pre-send simulation,
   so it never lands and costs nothing. The script prints
   `Transfer blocked: account is frozen`.
3. **Thaw.** `thawAccount(...)` unfreezes the account, and the same transfer
   goes through. The holder ends with 90 EXF.

Freezing only affects this one account: the holder's other tokens, and everyone
else's EXF, keep moving.
