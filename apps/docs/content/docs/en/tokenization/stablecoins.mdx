A stablecoin is a token redeemable for a reference currency. The mint is
normally a record of a liability, not the asset itself, so the reserve sits
off-chain and the register of record question in
[token design](/docs/tokenization/design) is the first one to settle.

## The sequence

1. **[Design the token](/docs/tokenization/design)** - confirm the mint records
   a claim rather than holding the reserve, then choose the token program and
   lay out the authorities that mint, freeze, and pause.
2. **[Choose transfer controls](/docs/tokenization/design/choosing-controls)** -
   which controls the asset actually needs, and which ones constrain the payment
   flows below.
3. **[Prove compliance](/docs/tokenization/compliance)** - binding a KYC or
   sanctions decision to an address without publishing personal data.
4. **[Gate the holders](/docs/tokenization/compliance/token-acl)** - allowlists
   and blocklists, where holder eligibility is restricted.
5. **[Integrate custody](/docs/tokenization/custody)** - what a custodian needs
   from the mint before it can hold or distribute the token.
6. **[Operate it](/docs/tokenization/tutorials)** - build a gated, operable mint
   and run the supply operations against it.

## Where it gets used

A stablecoin is usually issued in order to be paid with.
[Payments](/docs/payments) is the integration surface for accepting, sending,
and reconciling stablecoin flows, and
[choose the cash leg](/docs/tokenization/settlement/cash-leg) covers the case
where the stablecoin is the cash side of a settlement against another asset.
