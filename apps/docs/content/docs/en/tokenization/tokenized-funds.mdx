A tokenized fund issues shares priced at a periodic valuation point, whether
the net asset value (NAV) floats or is held stable. That pricing schedule is
what sets a fund apart: a subscription or redemption cannot complete until the
next price is struck.

## Register and holders

The share register can be the mint itself, or a transfer agent's register that
the mint mirrors; see
[is the token the asset, or a record of one?](/developers/docs/tokenization/design#is-the-token-the-asset-or-a-record-of-one)
A token balance usually equals the share count, with the NAV per share
published by the fund administrator or onchain.

Shares are usually limited to investors eligible for the share class. An
[attestation](/developers/docs/tokenization/compliance/attestation-based-compliance)
can carry that eligibility as a tier or category where holding limits differ by
investor class.

## The controls that matter most

- **A holder allowlist**, since every holder is an onboarded investor; see
  [allowlist or blocklist](/developers/docs/tokenization/design/choosing-controls#allowlist-or-blocklist).
- **Permissioned burn** for redemptions, so the fund co-signs each one. That
  makes the fund's own systems a liveness dependency for every redemption.
- **Scaled UI Amount** for distributed yield or share splits. It changes the
  displayed balance without a transfer, so reconcile raw amounts. The
  [DvP program](/developers/docs/defi/dvp-program#mint-configuration-constraints)
  does not accept a mint with this extension.
- **Forced transfer and pause**, as for any regulated asset; see
  [controls by asset type](/developers/docs/tokenization/design#controls-by-asset-type).

## Subscriptions and redemptions

Fund dealing accepts orders against a cut-off and prices them at the next
valuation point, so a vault or program that issues shares needs a
[request-and-settle design](/developers/docs/tokenization/custody/vault-architectures#the-request-and-settle-lifecycle)
rather than settling in one transaction. Decide what happens when a request
cannot settle, for example for lack of redemption liquidity or because the
investor left the allowlist before the strike.

[Choosing the cash leg](/developers/docs/defi/cash-leg) covers what
subscriptions are paid in and what redemptions pay out.
[Settlement and finality](/developers/docs/defi/finality) covers which
commitment level the fund's books should record.

## Worked example

[NAV strikes](/developers/docs/tokenization/nav-strikes) works through the
pricing cycle end to end, using a money market fund with several strikes a day.
The strike cadence, liquidity model, redemption timing, and regulatory
treatment in that example are specific to money market funds and may differ
for other fund structures.
