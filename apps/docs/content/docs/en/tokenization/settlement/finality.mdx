<Callout type="info" title="Summary">

Solana exposes three commitment levels. A system of record should mark a
transfer settled at `finalized`, which a block reaches once 31 further blocks
have been built on top of it - so the finalized head trails the tip of the chain
by at least 32 slots, the block itself plus the 31 above it, and by more when
slots are skipped. Every figure on this page counts blocks and slots that way.
An interface may show a user a result at `confirmed`, which is reached sooner.
The
[transaction confirmation & expiration](/developers/cookbook/transactions/confirmation)
page explains this in more detail.

</Callout>

## The three commitment levels

| Level       | Means                                                        | Reached after          | Can it be reversed?            |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| `processed` | The transaction is in a block produced by the current leader | 1 slot                 | Yes, routinely                 |
| `confirmed` | A supermajority of stake has voted for that block            | Typically 1 to 2 slots | Yes, in principle              |
| `finalized` | The block is rooted and a supermajority has built on it      | 31 further blocks      | Only by a supermajority attack |

### Express these in slots

Slot time is a protocol parameter currently changing, so any figure in
milliseconds has a short shelf life. **Record the slot count; derive the
wall-clock; state the slot time you assumed.**

As of September 2026, the mainnet target slot time is 300ms, the second of the
four staged decrements in
[SIMD-0525](https://github.com/solana-foundation/solana-improvement-documents/blob/main/proposals/0525-reduce-slot-times.md),
which reduces slot times from 400ms to 200ms. Each step has its own feature gate
and activates independently, so the remaining steps are not on a fixed
timetable. Read the current value from the live dashboard at
[solana.com/200ms](https://solana.com/200ms).

At 300ms per slot, 31 blocks with no skipped slots is approximately 9.3 seconds.
At 400ms it was approximately 12.4 seconds. The block count is unchanged; only
the derived wall-clock figure moved.

<Callout type="warn">

The `DEFAULT_MS_PER_SLOT` constant in the Solana SDK is a compile-time default
that lags the network: 400 in `solana-clock` 3.x, 300 in 4.0. A client that
computes elapsed time from the constant can return an incorrect figure with no
error. Read the current slot time from the cluster rather than from a constant,
and track the rollout at [solana.com/200ms](https://solana.com/200ms) or
[reduced slot times](/upgrades/reduced-slot-times) - but verify the activation
state against the feature gate itself, because published status pages can lag
the chain.

</Callout>

## Which level to use

**For a system of record, use `finalized`.** This is the level at which the
protocol's non-reversal property applies.

**For user-facing feedback, `confirmed` is appropriate.** It is reached quickly.
It does not support releasing an asset.

**Avoid `processed` for anything consequential.** It reflects a single leader's
view and is routinely superseded.

A treasury system that credits a balance at `confirmed` takes settlement risk on
that credit; displaying the same result in an interface does not create an
exposure.

### What finality completes depends on your register of record

Where the mint is the register of record, a finalized transfer updates that
record and no separate book has to be updated. Whether the entitlement has moved
as a matter of law is the legal-finality question below.

Where the token records an asset held elsewhere, a finalized transfer settles
the onchain leg only. The off-chain register still has to be updated, and the
interval in which the token and the off-chain register disagree needs a named
owner and a documented procedure for the case where the second update does not
happen. This holds even where the trade settled atomically against cash: the two
token legs move together, and the register update sits outside that transaction.
See [token design](/docs/tokenization/design) for the distinction and
[choosing the cash leg](/docs/tokenization/settlement/cash-leg) for what the
atomic transaction does and does not complete.

### Blockhash commitment and the validity window

A transaction is valid only while the blockhash it references is recent - the
blockhash is valid for 150 blocks (about 150 slots when none are skipped), and
the runtime rejects anything older. If you fetch that blockhash at `finalized`,
you are starting from a block that is already at least 32 slots old, so roughly
a fifth of the 150-block window is gone before you have signed anything.

**Fetch the blockhash at `confirmed`** so the transaction gets its full window,
and use `finalized` for deciding whether the result counts. The two uses are
independent.

Where observed slot times exceed the target, a wall-clock window computed from
the target understates the time available. Read the observed slot time from
[solana.com/200ms](https://solana.com/200ms) before sizing any slack, and
rescale it as slot times change.

## Protocol finality is not legal finality

**Protocol finality** is the property described above: the conditions under
which the network will not reorganize a block. It is defined by the protocol and
publicly observable.

**Legal finality** is whether a transfer is irrevocable and
unwindable-only-by-exception under the law of a particular jurisdiction,
typically in insolvency. It depends on the venue, the governing law, the legal
characterization of the token, and in many jurisdictions on whether the system
is designated under a settlement finality regime.

The protocol establishes when a transaction is final on the ledger. Whether a
transfer is final in law is decided outside it, and regimes may treat settlement
finality differently, so the question is answered for each jurisdiction of
settlement.

In practice: state the protocol property, cite it, and refer the legal question
to counsel in each relevant jurisdiction.

## What a reorg can and cannot do

Blocks that have not been finalized can be superseded. This is normal operation
and systems reading the chain should expect it.

- **Before `finalized`**, a transaction you have seen may end up on a fork that
  is abandoned. The transaction did not fail; the block containing it was
  superseded by one the network built on instead.
- **After `finalized`**, superseding a block would require a supermajority of
  stake to act against the protocol.

Record a superseded transaction as superseded rather than deleting it. Keeping
the history makes it possible to explain later why an entry that once existed no
longer appears.

## Ordering

Order events by **slot**, not by block time. Block time is a validator-reported
estimate and is not monotonic across the cluster. Slot numbers are the ledger's
own sequence and are unambiguous.

Where two instructions in the same transaction both affect your books, key your
records on the transaction signature together with the instruction index. A
signature alone is not a unique key for a ledger entry.

## For a regulatory submission

The statements below cover Solana's settlement properties. Each is independently
checkable:

1. Three commitment levels exist, with the meanings above.
2. Finality is reached once 31 further blocks have been built on the block, so
   the finalized head trails the tip by at least 32 slots. Slot time is a
   protocol parameter, 300ms as of September 2026 and subject to further staged
   reduction under SIMD-0525, so state the slot time you assumed alongside any
   wall-clock figure.
3. Finality rests on economic assumptions about the distribution of stake, and
   is probabilistic in that sense.
4. Legal finality is a separate question determined by jurisdiction, and the
   protocol makes no claim about it.

For network liveness history, validator counts, and concentration measures, take
current values from [status.solana.com](https://status.solana.com),
[validators.app](https://www.validators.app), and
[solana.com/data](https://solana.com/data) rather than from documentation.
