<Callout type="info" title="Summary">

Both legs of a trade can be accounts on the same ledger, so one transaction can
exchange them or leave both positions unchanged. The decision is which cash
instrument settles against the asset: a stablecoin, a token representing a
deposit at a bank, or a payment on existing rails recorded onchain. That choice
and the mint's controls constrain each other.

</Callout>

## Atomic exchange in one transaction

The runtime applies every instruction in a transaction or none of them. Where
the asset and the cash are both token accounts on Solana, a single transaction
moves the asset one way and the cash the other, and either both transfers take
effect or neither does. No clearing house is needed between the legs, though a
settlement agent or authority still signs (below). Two patterns use the
property: the [DvP program](/docs/tokenization/settlement/dvp-program) holds
each leg in a program-owned escrow and lets a named settlement authority settle
both, and the [extensions-only pattern](/docs/tokenization/settlement/dvp)
settles a Token-2022 asset against a stablecoin through delegated authority,
with no custom program.

Atomicity covers the two onchain legs only. Where an off-chain register governs,
its update is a separate step; see
[settlement and finality](/docs/tokenization/settlement/finality#finality-and-the-register-of-record).

### Signing authority for both legs

A transaction that moves two positions needs authority over both. In the
extensions-only pattern, each side approves a delegate for its own leg and a
settlement agent signs the combined transaction, holding neither position at any
point. The issuer can also act on the asset leg through a permanent delegate, or
one party can sign both legs against a delegation granted in advance.

Two constraints follow. A token account carries one delegate at a time, so a
position pledged to one settlement arrangement is not simultaneously available
to another. And whoever signs is an operational dependency, in the same way as
the mint authorities. See
[choosing controls](/docs/tokenization/design/choosing-controls#split-authorities-from-the-start).

## Choosing the cash instrument

| Instrument                             | Atomic with the asset leg?       | Provides                                                                                              | Considerations                                                                |
| -------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Stablecoin**                         | Yes                              | Wider counterparty reach than a deposit token; no specific bank relationship needed                   | Issuer credit, redemption and freeze exposure while held; a credit assessment |
| **Tokenized deposit**                  | Yes                              | A deposit claim on a specific bank, held inside an existing deposit relationship                      | May be limited to the bank's customers; counterparties must pass both gates   |
| **Off-ledger rail, confirmed onchain** | No; cash moves on existing rails | Existing payment infrastructure, correspondent relationships, and cash controls stay in use unchanged | Legs can diverge; a reference recorded onchain links them                     |

Deposit tokens from two banks are not fungible: a venue that accepts more than
one introduces a conversion between two claims denominated in the same currency.
This is known as singleness of money; the settlement mechanism does not resolve
its operational, legal, and accounting aspects.

Stablecoin and deposit-token regimes may differ by jurisdiction, including on
whether an issuer may pay or pass through yield.

## Mint controls and the cash leg

The mint's controls and the cash instrument limit each other:

| Control                       | Effect on settlement                                                                                                                                            | Effect in the other direction                                                                              |
| ----------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Frozen by default + Token ACL | A counterparty needs a thawed account before the exchange. A frozen destination fails the whole transaction, including the cash leg                             | Permissionless thaw lets a counterparty qualify without the issuer signing for each one                    |
| Transfer hook                 | The transaction carries the hook's extra accounts and compute, and venues that do not support hooks cannot settle the asset                                     | Where the cash leg needs broad venue support, an allowlist can express the rule instead                    |
| Permanent delegate            | Gives the issuer a way to move the asset leg without a counterparty delegation                                                                                  | Reduces the pressure on the single-delegate limit, at the cost of a key that can move any holder's balance |
| Confidential Balances         | Hides amounts on the asset leg, while a public stablecoin cash leg still shows the payment; on the DvP program path the escrowed and settled amounts are public | A confidential cash leg narrows the instruments available, since it has to be supported on both sides      |
| Pause                         | A paused asset leg fails the transaction, and a counterparty may have committed cash intent before that happens                                                 | The pause authority has to be reachable during settlement windows, not only during incidents               |
