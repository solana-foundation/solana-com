<Callout type="info" title="Summary">

Settlement has two legs: the asset and the cash. Decide which cash instrument
carries the cash leg, decide whether both legs must move in the same
transaction, and decide which commitment level your books treat as settled.
These three decisions are independent of each other.

</Callout>

## Payment, settlement, and finality

These three are often used interchangeably and mean different things:

- **Payment execution** - moving value from one party to another. Documented in
  [Payments](/docs/payments).
- **Settlement** - discharging both sides of an obligation, so the asset and the
  cash both end up where they belong.
- **Finality** - the point at which the ledger will not reverse the transfer,
  which is a different question from whether your records call it settled.

## The decisions

1. **[Choose the cash leg](/docs/tokenization/settlement/cash-leg)** - a
   stablecoin, a tokenized deposit, or an off-ledger rail. This choice
   constrains which controls you can put on the mint, and the controls on the
   mint constrain the choice, so settle both together.
2. **[Delivery versus payment](/docs/tokenization/settlement/dvp)** - exchanging
   the asset against its payment in a single transaction, so neither side is
   exposed to the other having failed to deliver. Only available when both legs
   are on the same ledger.
3. **[Settlement and finality](/docs/tokenization/settlement/finality)** - what
   `processed`, `confirmed`, and `finalized` mean, how many slots each takes,
   and which one a system of record should trust.

## Atomic and non-atomic settlement

If both legs are onchain, a single transaction either completes both or neither,
and there is no window in which one party has delivered and the other has not.
That property belongs to the transaction, not to any program.

If the cash leg is off-ledger, no transaction can span both, and settlement
becomes a reconciliation problem instead: the asset leg is recorded onchain, the
cash leg is recorded on your existing rail, and something has to tie them
together and handle the case where one succeeds and the other does not.
[Choose the cash leg](/docs/tokenization/settlement/cash-leg) works through what
each option requires.

## Protocol state is not accounting state

A transfer that is `finalized` is final on the ledger. Whether it is settled for
your books depends on where your register of record lives and what your
counterparties have acknowledged - which is why
[settlement and finality](/docs/tokenization/settlement/finality) treats those
as separate questions, and why
[custody integration](/docs/tokenization/custody/custody-integration) covers
reconciling against a custodian's own view.
