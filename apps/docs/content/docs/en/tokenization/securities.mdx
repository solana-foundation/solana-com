A tokenized security carries a register of record and a transfer agent, and its
holders are restricted by rule rather than by preference. Those two facts drive
the sequence below.

## The sequence

1. **[Settle the register of record](/docs/tokenization/design)** - whether the
   mint is the register or mirrors one held off-chain. This is the decision that
   is hardest to revisit after launch.
2. **[Design the authorities](/docs/tokenization/design/program-and-authority-design)** -
   which key or multisig holds each authority, and how that maps onto the
   transfer agent's duties.
3. **[Choose transfer controls](/docs/tokenization/design/choosing-controls)** -
   the control surface an instrument with restricted holders needs.
4. **[Prove holder eligibility](/docs/tokenization/compliance)** - attestations
   that evidence an eligibility decision without publishing the underlying data,
   and [Token ACL](/docs/tokenization/compliance/token-acl) to enforce it.
5. **[Integrate custody](/docs/tokenization/custody)** - the custodian
   relationship and the
   [issuer and transfer-agent boundary](/docs/tokenization/custody/transfer-agent-responsibilities).
6. **[Settle against payment](/docs/tokenization/settlement/dvp)** - exchanging
   the security against its cash leg in a single transaction.
7. **[Operate it](/docs/tokenization/tutorials)** - build a gated, operable mint
   and run the ongoing operations against it.

## Settlement

[Delivery versus payment](/docs/tokenization/settlement/dvp) covers the two-leg
exchange itself. [Choose the cash leg](/docs/tokenization/settlement/cash-leg)
covers which cash instrument settles against the security and how that choice
and the controls on the mint constrain each other.
[Settlement and finality](/docs/tokenization/settlement/finality) covers which
commitment level to record as settled.
