A tokenized fund issues shares priced at a periodic valuation point, whether the
NAV floats or is held stable. The pricing schedule affects several of the
decisions below.

## The sequence

1. **[Design the share](/docs/tokenization/design)** - whether the mint or an
   off-chain register is the register of record, and where the share price is
   published.
2. **[Evidence compliance](/docs/tokenization/compliance)** - eligibility for
   the share class, evidenced without publishing holder data.
3. **[Gate the holders](/docs/tokenization/compliance/token-acl)** - restricting
   who may subscribe or hold.
4. **[Integrate custody](/docs/tokenization/custody)** - the custodian and
   transfer-agent boundary, including
   [vault architectures](/docs/tokenization/custody/vault-architectures) where
   shares are held in a vault.
5. **[Choose the cash leg](/docs/tokenization/settlement/cash-leg)** - what
   subscriptions are paid in and what redemptions pay out.
6. **[Run the pricing cycle](/docs/tokenization/tokenized-funds/nav-strikes)** -
   striking a net asset value and processing subscriptions and redemptions
   against it.

## Fund operations

A token balance usually equals the share count, with NAV per share published by
the fund administrator or onchain.

[NAV strikes](/docs/tokenization/tokenized-funds/nav-strikes) works the pricing
cycle through end to end using a money market fund as its example. The strike
cadence, liquidity model, redemption timing, and regulatory treatment in that
example are specific to money market funds and may differ for other fund
structures.

[Settlement and finality](/docs/tokenization/settlement/finality) covers which
commitment level a fund's books should record.

See [Assets](/docs/tokens) for the token programs and the extension reference.
