A standard Solana payment is public: once it is confirmed, anyone can look up
the amount, the sender, and recipient accounts, and their balances. Three
solutions keep parts of a payment private. They differ in what they hide, who
can audit it, and which tokens they work with.

<Info>
  **Decide what has to stay private**

  "Private" means something different in each solution. Before you choose,
  decide which parts of a payment must be hidden (the amount, balances, the
  sender and recipient, or business data such as memos) and who still needs to
  see them, such as an auditor or the issuer.
</Info>

## Compare the three solutions

Confidential Balances is available on mainnet. The Solana Privacy Rings and
Private Channels are coming, but are not yet ready for production.

| Solution              | Amount and balances                      | Sender and recipient                                   | Audit access                                                           | Tokens                                                             | Availability                                         |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Confidential Balances | Encrypted                                | Public                                                 | Optional auditor key on the mint decrypts transfer amounts             | Token-2022 mints that enabled the extension when they were created | Available on mainnet                                 |
| Solana Privacy Rings  | Asset and amount private                 | Public in the Default Ring, private in anonymous Rings | Custom Rings can give an auditor visibility; the Default Ring has none | SOL, SPL tokens, and Token-2022 tokens                             | Coming soon: in beta, live on devnet                 |
| Private Channels      | Hidden from outside observers            | Hidden from outside observers                          | The institution controls who participates, what rules apply and how data is accessed | SPL tokens the channel admin allows                                | Coming soon: not yet security audited                |

For trades rather than payments, see
[Confidential settlement](/developers/docs/defi/confidential-settlement).

## Confidential Balances

Confidential Balances is a Token-2022 extension that encrypts token balances and
transfer amounts. Payments still move between ordinary, public token accounts.

- **Private in a payment:** the transfer amount and the encrypted balances of
  both accounts.
- **Public in a payment:** the mint, the token accounts, their owners, and the
  fact that the sender paid the recipient. This is confidentiality from public
  observers, not anonymity. Memos and other instruction data are not encrypted.
- **Moving in and out:** deposits from an account's public balance into its
  confidential balance, and withdrawals back out, carry their amounts in the
  clear.
- **Audit model:** the mint can set an optional auditor ElGamal key. The holder
  of the auditor secret key can decrypt every transfer amount for that mint, but
  not an account's full balance, and the key gives no authority to move tokens.
- **Sending and receiving:** both accounts must be configured for confidential
  transfers. The sender's client generates zero-knowledge proofs, which the ZK
  ElGamal Proof Program verifies on Solana, and the recipient applies its
  pending balance before it can spend what it received.

### Which tokens support it

The extension must be set up when the mint is created and cannot be added to an
existing mint. A token on the original SPL Token program has no extensions, so
it cannot use Confidential Balances.

Major stablecoin mints on mainnet at the time of writing (October 2026):

| Stablecoin | Mint                                           | Token program | Confidential Balances                                  |
| ---------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| USDC       | `EPjFWdd5AufqSSqeM2qN1xzybapC8G4wEGGkZwyTDt1v` | SPL Token     | Not supported                                          |
| USDT       | `Es9vMFrzaCERmJfrF4H2FYD4KCoNkY11McCe8BenwNYB` | SPL Token     | Not supported                                          |
| PYUSD      | `2b1kV6DkPAnxd5ixfnxCpjxmKwqjjaYmCZfHsFu24GXo` | Token-2022    | Extension enabled, manual approval, no auditor key set |
| USDG       | `2u1tszSeqZ3qBWF3uNGPFc8TzMk2tdiwknnRMWGWjGWH` | Token-2022    | Extension enabled, manual approval, no auditor key set |

The PYUSD and USDG mints also carry the permanent delegate extension and the
transfer fee extension, with the fee set to 0%.

With manual approval, an account can make confidential transfers only after the
mint's Confidential Balances authority approves it, so whether you can pay in
PYUSD or USDG confidentially depends on the issuer approving your accounts.

### Set up confidential payments

If you issue the token your payments use:

1. **Enable it when you create the mint.** Add the Confidential Balances
   extension at creation; it can't be added later. See
   [create a confidential mint](/developers/docs/tokens/extensions/confidential-transfer/issuer-guide#create-a-confidential-mint),
   or the
   [Enable confidentiality (optional)](/developers/docs/assets/issue-asset/local#enable-confidentiality-optional)
   step of the Issue an asset tutorial.
2. **Approve accounts.** With a manual approve policy, approve each account
   after your own checks, such as KYC, before it can pay confidentially. See
   [approve accounts](/developers/docs/tokens/extensions/confidential-transfer/issuer-guide#approve-accounts-manual-policy).
3. **Set an auditor key.** Give an auditor, such as your compliance team or a
   regulator, the ability to decrypt transfer amounts. See
   [configure an auditor](/developers/docs/tokens/extensions/confidential-transfer/issuer-guide#configure-an-auditor).
4. **Support it in your payment flow.** Configure the payer's and recipient's
   token accounts, apply pending balances when payments arrive, and show both
   public and confidential balances. See the
   [integration guide](/developers/docs/tokens/extensions/confidential-transfer/integration-guide).

[Learn how Confidential Balances works](/developers/docs/tokens/extensions/confidential-transfer).

## Solana Privacy Rings

Coming soon. Solana Privacy Rings provide encrypted onchain balances and
private transfers. They are in beta and not yet ready for production: Helius
describes them as live on devnet and
undergoing independent security audits and formal verification. In the
permissionless Default Ring, the asset and amount of a payment are private and
the sender and recipient are public. Custom Rings add policy and auditor
controls, and can also hide the sender and recipient.

[Read the Solana Privacy Rings documentation](https://www.helius.dev/docs/privacy/)
or [try the Helius beta demo](https://helius.dev/privacy/demo) (devnet, not for
production use).

## Private Channels

Coming soon. Private Channels is private payment infrastructure that an
institution runs: users deposit tokens into an escrow program on mainnet, pay
each other inside the channel, and withdraw back to mainnet. Payments inside the
channel are not recorded on mainnet, so outside observers can't see them. The
institution controls who participates, what rules apply, and how data is
accessed. It has not been security audited and is not
recommended for production use with real funds.

[Explore Private Channels](/developers/docs/tools/private-channels) or
[view the product page](https://launch.solana.com/products/private-channels).
