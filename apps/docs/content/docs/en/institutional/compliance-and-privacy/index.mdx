<Callout type="info" title="Summary">

Two different problems share this section. One is proving a check happened
without publishing what it was based on, which attestations solve. The other is
keeping amounts confidential, which the confidential transfer extension solves.
Neither hides who transacted with whom, and the second one takes away any rule
that depends on the size of a transfer.

</Callout>

## Two problems, often confused

**Proving a decision.** A gated token needs an onchain answer to "may this
address hold the token?" The inputs to that answer - identity documents,
screening hits, beneficial-ownership records - are personal data, and Solana's
ledger is public and permanent. Attestations let the check run where it always
ran and publish only its conclusion.

**Concealing a value.** A separate requirement, and a separate mechanism.
Confidential balances encrypt amounts and balances while leaving addresses, the
mint, and the timing of every transfer public.

They compose, but they are not substitutes. Attestations do not hide anything
about the transfer; confidential balances prove nothing about the holder.

## What neither one does

Neither conceals the transaction graph. Addresses, counterparties, and the fact
that a transfer occurred stay readable in both designs. If the counterparty
relationships are themselves the sensitive thing, that is a question about where
the asset lives rather than how the mint is configured. No option on Solana
conceals the graph by default; compare what each one hides in
[Privacy on Solana](/docs/finance/privacy), then see the
[deployment decision guide](/docs/institutional/architecture/deployment-decision-guide).

<Callout type="warn">

Confidential balances cannot be added after the mint is created, and any policy
conditioned on transfer size stops being enforceable onchain once amounts are
encrypted. Both belong in the design decision, not a later phase.

</Callout>
