<Callout type="info" title="Summary">

Both legs of a trade can be accounts on the same ledger, so one transaction can
exchange them or leave both positions unchanged. What remains a decision is
which cash instrument settles against the asset: a stablecoin, a token
representing a deposit at a bank, or a payment on existing rails recorded
onchain. That choice and the controls on the mint constrain each other in both
directions.

</Callout>

## Atomic exchange is a property of the ledger

The runtime applies every instruction in a transaction or none of them. Where
the asset and the cash are both token accounts on Solana, a single transaction
moves the asset one way and the cash the other, and either both transfers take
effect or neither does. No clearing intermediary sits between the legs, and no
custom program is required to achieve it -
[delivery versus payment](/docs/tokenization/dvp) settles a Token-2022 asset
against standard USDC using extensions alone.

### Someone still has to sign both legs

A transaction that moves two positions needs authority over both. Commonly each
side approves a delegate for its own leg and a settlement agent signs the
combined transaction, holding neither position at any point. The issuer can also
act on the asset leg through a permanent delegate, or one party can sign both
legs against a delegation granted in advance.

Two constraints follow. A token account carries one delegate at a time, so a
position pledged to one settlement arrangement is not simultaneously available
to another. And whoever signs is an operational dependency: their key custody,
availability, and authorization evidence belong in the same controls review as
the mint authorities. See
[choosing controls](/docs/institutional/token-design/choosing-controls#split-authorities-from-the-start).

## Choosing the cash instrument

### Settle in a stablecoin

Both legs are tokens on the same ledger, and the exchange is atomic as described
above.

- **What you take on.** Exposure to the stablecoin issuer, its reserves, its
  redemption terms, and its ability to freeze balances. Both parties take that
  exposure. Whether it is acceptable is a credit assessment.
- **What it offers.** The widest set of counterparties, since holding the cash
  leg requires no relationship with a particular bank.

### Settle in a tokenized deposit

The cash leg is a token representing a deposit claim on a specific bank. It
settles atomically in exactly the same way as a stablecoin, but the claim is on
a supervised institution and typically sits inside an existing deposit
relationship.

- **The trade-off is reach.** A deposit token is usually restricted to
  counterparties who are already customers of the issuing bank, so it settles
  within a network rather than across the market. A counterparty must satisfy
  the gate on your asset and the gate on the bank's cash token, and the two
  lists are maintained by different institutions.
- **Two deposit tokens from two banks are not fungible.** A venue that accepts
  more than one introduces a conversion between two claims denominated in the
  same currency. This is the singleness-of-money problem. It has operational,
  legal, and accounting dimensions, is not resolved by the settlement mechanism,
  and is one of the topics
  [risk and diligence](/docs/institutional/risk-and-diligence) flags as one
  where no specific standard-setter guidance has been located.

### Settle off-ledger, confirm onchain

The asset leg moves onchain; the cash leg moves through your existing rails. A
reference recorded with the onchain transfer ties the two together for
reconciliation. This forgoes the atomicity described above.

- **What you keep.** Your existing payment infrastructure, correspondent
  relationships, and cash controls, unchanged.
- **What you give up.** Atomicity. The two legs can diverge, so a documented
  procedure for that case is needed.
- **When it is right.** Large-value trades where the cash leg already settles
  through a central bank system, and pilots where changing the cash
  infrastructure is out of scope.

## Controls and the cash leg constrain each other

The control surface chosen for the mint determines what can settle against it,
and the cash instrument you need places limits on how restrictive that surface
can be.

| Control                       | Effect on settlement                                                                                                                | Effect in the other direction                                                                                       |
| ----------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Frozen by default + Token ACL | A counterparty needs a thawed account before the exchange. A frozen destination fails the whole transaction, including the cash leg | Permissionless thaw lets a counterparty qualify without the issuer signing for each one                             |
| Transfer hook                 | The transaction carries the hook's extra accounts and compute, and venues that do not support hooks cannot settle the asset at all  | A cash leg that depends on broad venue support argues for expressing the rule as a list where a list can express it |
| Permanent delegate            | Gives the issuer a way to move the asset leg without a counterparty delegation                                                      | Reduces the pressure on the single-delegate limit, at the cost of a key that can move any holder's balance          |
| Confidential balances         | Hides amounts on the asset leg, while a public stablecoin cash leg still shows the payment                                          | A confidential cash leg narrows the instruments available, since it has to be supported on both sides               |
| Pause                         | A paused asset leg fails the transaction, and a counterparty may have committed cash intent before that happens                     | The pause authority has to be reachable during settlement windows, not only during incidents                        |

## What atomic settlement completes

Where the mint is the register of record, a finalized atomic transaction is the
settlement. Both entitlements have moved and nothing further is required.

Where the token records an asset held elsewhere, the same transaction settles
the token leg and the cash leg atomically, but the off-chain register is not
part of that guarantee. Atomicity covers the two onchain legs and stops there,
so the register update remains a separate step that can fail on its own. See
[token design decisions](/docs/institutional/token-design) for the distinction
and [settlement and finality](/docs/institutional/settlement-finality) for the
reconciliation exposure this leaves and who has to own it.

## Choosing

| If                                                                           | Then                                                                          |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Counterparties are outside your institution and reach matters most           | Stablecoin                                                                    |
| Counterparties are your own clients and balance-sheet treatment matters most | Tokenized deposit                                                             |
| The cash leg must stay on existing rails                                     | Off-ledger with onchain confirmation                                          |
| You need atomicity but cannot take issuer credit risk                        | Tokenized deposit, or reconsider whether atomicity is the binding requirement |

## What to settle before you build

- **Which balance sheet holds the cash**, and what your treasury policy says
  about holding it there.
- **Who signs each leg**, which account is delegated to whom at the point of
  settlement, and what that delegation blocks while it stands.
- **What happens on a failed leg**, in each direction, with a named owner.
- **Which commitment level marks the trade settled in the books**, which may
  differ from the level an interface displays. See
  [settlement and finality](/docs/institutional/settlement-finality).
- **How the cash leg is funded intraday**, and whether that funding is itself
  atomic.

<Callout type="warn">

Stablecoin and deposit-token regimes differ by jurisdiction and are changing.
Confirm the treatment of a chosen cash leg with counsel for each relevant
jurisdiction, including whether an issuer may pay or pass through yield, before
committing to a design.

</Callout>
