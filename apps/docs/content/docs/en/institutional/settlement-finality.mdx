<Callout type="info" title="Summary">

Solana exposes three commitment levels. A system of record should mark a
transfer settled at `finalized`, which is reached 31 slots after the block that
contained it - so the finalized head trails the tip of the chain by 32 slots.
Your interface may show a user a result at `confirmed`, which is much faster.
The
[transaction confirmation & expiration](/developers/cookbook/transactions/confirmation)
page explains this in more detail.

</Callout>

## The three commitment levels

| Level       | Means                                                        | Reached after          | Can it be reversed?            |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| `processed` | The transaction is in a block produced by the current leader | 1 slot                 | Yes, routinely                 |
| `confirmed` | A supermajority of stake has voted for that block            | Typically 1 to 2 slots | Yes, in principle              |
| `finalized` | The block is rooted and a supermajority has built on it      | 31 slots               | Only by a supermajority attack |

### Express these in slots, not seconds

Slot time is a protocol parameter that is actively changing, so any figure in
milliseconds has a short shelf life. **Record the slot count; derive the
wall-clock; state the slot time you assumed.**

As of August 2026, target slot time is 350ms, following the first step of
[SIMD-0525](https://github.com/solana-foundation/solana-improvement-documents/pull/525),
which reduces slot times from 400ms to 200ms in four staged decrements. Each
step has its own feature gate and activates independently, so the remaining
steps are not on a fixed timetable. Read the current value from the live
dashboard at [solana.com/200ms](https://solana.com/200ms).

At 350ms per slot, 31 slots is approximately 10.9 seconds. At 400ms it was
approximately 12.4 seconds. The slot count is unchanged; only the derived
wall-clock figure moved.

<Callout type="warn">

The `DEFAULT_MS_PER_SLOT` constant in the Solana SDK is still 400. Any client
that computes elapsed time as `slots x 400ms` is now silently wrong. Read the
current slot time from the cluster rather than from a constant, and track the
rollout at [solana.com/200ms](https://solana.com/200ms) or
[reduced slot times](/upgrades/reduced-slot-times) - but verify the activation
state against the feature gate itself, because published status pages can lag
the chain.

</Callout>

## Which level to use

**For a system of record, use `finalized`.** This is the level at which the
network's guarantee applies. No finalized transaction has been reversed on
Solana mainnet-beta.

**For user-facing feedback, `confirmed` is appropriate.** It is fast enough to
feel immediate, and the risk it carries is acceptable for showing someone that
their action worked. It is not acceptable for releasing an asset against it.

**Avoid `processed` for anything consequential.** It reflects a single leader's
view and is routinely superseded.

A treasury system that credits a balance at `confirmed` is taking settlement
risk. An interface that displays a result at `confirmed` is not.

### What finality completes depends on your register of record

Where the mint is the register of record, a finalized transfer is the
settlement. Nothing further has to happen for the entitlement to have moved.

Where the token records an asset held elsewhere, a finalized transfer settles
the onchain leg only. The off-chain register still has to be updated, and the
interval between the two is a reconciliation exposure that needs a named owner
and a documented procedure for the case where the second update does not happen.
This holds even where the trade settled atomically against cash: the two token
legs move together, and the register update sits outside that transaction. See
[token design decisions](/docs/institutional/token-design) for the distinction
and
[payments and interoperability](/docs/institutional/payments-and-interoperability)
for what the atomic transaction does and does not complete.

### A practical trap when building transactions

A transaction is valid only while the blockhash it references is recent - the
network keeps the 150 most recent blockhashes and rejects anything older. If you
fetch that blockhash at `finalized`, you are starting from a block that is
already 32 slots old, so roughly a fifth of the 150-slot window is gone before
you have signed anything.

**Fetch the blockhash at `confirmed`** so the transaction gets its full window,
and use `finalized` for deciding whether the result counts. The two uses are
independent.

Note also that real slots run slightly longer than the target, so a window
computed from the target slot time is an optimistic estimate. Against a 350ms
target, mainnet slots sampled in August 2026 averaged closer to 365ms; read the
current figure from [solana.com/200ms](https://solana.com/200ms). Carry the same
slack proportionally as slot times change, and measure it rather than assuming
the target.

## Protocol finality is not legal finality

**Protocol finality** is the property described above: the conditions under
which the network will not reorganize a block. It is precisely defined, publicly
observable, and the same for everyone.

**Legal finality** is whether a transfer is irrevocable and unwindable-only-by-
exception under the law of a particular jurisdiction, typically in insolvency.
It depends on the venue, the governing law, the legal characterization of the
token, and in many jurisdictions on whether the system is designated under a
settlement finality regime.

Solana can tell you when a transaction is final under the protocol. It cannot
tell you when a transfer is final under the law, and no chain can. No technical
mitigant closes that gap. Each regulatory regime treats settlement finality
differently, so the question has to be answered for each jurisdiction you settle
in - see [risk and diligence](/docs/institutional/risk-and-diligence).

In practice: state the protocol property precisely, cite it, and treat the legal
question as a separate item for review in each relevant jurisdiction.

## What a reorg can and cannot do

Blocks that have not been finalized can be superseded. This is normal operation
and systems reading the chain should expect it.

- **Before `finalized`**, a transaction you have seen may end up on a fork that
  is abandoned. The transaction did not fail; the block containing it was
  superseded by one the network built on instead.
- **After `finalized`**, superseding a block would require a supermajority of
  stake to act against the protocol.

Record a superseded transaction as superseded rather than deleting it. Keeping
the history makes it possible to explain, months later, why an entry that once
existed no longer appears.

## Ordering

Order events by **slot**, not by block time. Block time is a validator-reported
estimate and is not monotonic across the cluster. Slot numbers are the ledger's
own sequence and are unambiguous.

Where two instructions in the same transaction both affect your books, key your
records on the transaction signature together with the instruction index. A
signature alone is not a unique key for a ledger entry.

## For a regulatory submission

The statements below are the ones typically needed when documenting Solana's
settlement properties for a supervisor. Each is independently checkable:

1. Three commitment levels exist, with the meanings above.
2. Finality is reached at 31 slots. Slot time is a protocol parameter, 350ms as
   of August 2026 and subject to further staged reduction under SIMD-0525, so
   state the slot time you assumed alongside any wall-clock figure.
3. No finalized transaction has been reversed on mainnet-beta.
4. Finality rests on economic assumptions about the distribution of stake, and
   is probabilistic in that sense.
5. Legal finality is a separate question determined by jurisdiction, and the
   protocol makes no claim about it.

For network liveness history, validator counts, and concentration measures - the
other figures a supervisor usually asks for alongside these - see
[risk and diligence](/docs/institutional/risk-and-diligence) and the live
sources in [resources](/docs/institutional/resources).
