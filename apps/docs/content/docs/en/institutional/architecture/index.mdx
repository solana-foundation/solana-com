<Callout type="info" title="Summary">

Start at mainnet and move away from it only when a requirement you can name
forces you to. Each step away buys confidentiality or control and pays for it in
composability, integration work, and product maturity. Most institutional
issuance is met without leaving the public network.

</Callout>

## The two questions

**Where does the asset live?** The rungs run from an ordinary mint on the public
network up to a Solana Permissioned Environment run independently of
mainnet-beta, and each rung costs you something the one below it gave you for
free. Work down the ladder and stop at the first rung that meets your
requirement.

**What settles against what, and when?** On mainnet every transfer is its own
settlement and the question does not arise. Inside a payment channel it does,
and the netting and net-settlement patterns built on that idea are still designs
under discussion rather than specified architecture.

The second question only matters if the first one takes you off mainnet.

## Where the constraint usually comes from

The choice is rarely made on the architecture's merits alone. Three things
narrow it before you start:

- **What your counterparties can hold.** A venue or custodian that does not
  support your extensions constrains the lower rungs, which is why extension
  support is worth confirming before anything else - see
  [custody integration](/docs/institutional/custody-and-vaults/custody-integration).
- **What has to stay confidential.** Amounts, the counterparty graph, and
  participation are three different requirements with three different answers.
  [Privacy on Solana](/docs/finance/privacy) is the authoritative comparison of
  the models that answer them - Confidential Balances, the Solana Privacy
  Protocol, and Private Channels - and answering this before you start narrows
  the ladder below. For the issuer-facing detail on the first of the three, see
  [privacy and transfer controls](/docs/institutional/compliance-and-privacy/privacy-and-transfer-controls).
- **What is actually deployable today.** The upper rungs are products still
  taking shape. The decision guide labels each one so a launch is not planned
  around something that is not there yet.
