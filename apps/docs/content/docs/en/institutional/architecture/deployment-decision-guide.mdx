<Callout type="info" title="Summary">

Start at mainnet and escalate only when a named requirement forces you up a
rung. Each step buys confidentiality or control and pays for it in
composability, integration work, and - past the first two rungs - product
maturity. Most institutional issuance requirements are met at rung 1 or 2.

</Callout>

## How to read this page

The rungs are ordered by how much they take you away from the public network.
Each rung up is a cost accepted to meet a requirement you can name. Work down
this list and stop at the first rung that meets your requirement.

Rungs 2 through 4 are the three privacy models compared in
[Privacy on Solana](/docs/finance/privacy), which is the authoritative account
of what each one conceals. Settle that question there first; this page decides
where the asset lives once you know which model you need.

Because the upper rungs are products still taking shape, each is labeled:

- **Available** - deployable today, with documented mechanics.
- **In development** - real, being built, but not something to design a
  production launch around without talking to the team behind it.
- **By arrangement** - check availability and current capabilities are discussed
  directly with the Solana Foundation.

## Rung 1 - Mainnet, transparent

**Available.**

The default. Your token is an ordinary Token-2022 mint on the public network,
with the control surface from
[choosing controls](/docs/institutional/token-design/choosing-controls).

You get the whole ecosystem: every wallet, custodian, venue, indexer, and
oracle, plus atomic settlement against any other asset on the same ledger
without a bridge. You also get a public ledger, where balances, counterparties,
and amounts are all readable.

**Stay here if** your asset's holdings and flows are not confidential, or you
have confirmed with counsel that the obligation is met notwithstanding that
addresses are pseudonymous rather than anonymous.

## Rung 2 - Mainnet with confidential balances

**Available.**

The same mint with the
[confidential transfer extension](/docs/tokens/extensions/confidential-transfer)
enabled. [Privacy on Solana](/docs/finance/privacy) sets out what Confidential
Balances hides and what it leaves public.

The proof system these depend on is enabled on mainnet-beta, so this rung is
deployable. Adoption remains limited, and account-level controls compose cleanly
here while any amount-dependent rule does not. Both qualifications are covered
in
[privacy and transfer controls](/docs/institutional/compliance-and-privacy/privacy-and-transfer-controls).

**Move here if** position sizes or payment amounts are commercially sensitive or
confidential.

**This rung does not conceal the transaction graph.** If that is your
requirement, keep reading.

## Rung 3 - Mainnet with the Solana Privacy Protocol

**In development.**

The Solana Privacy Protocol - summarized in
[Privacy on Solana](/docs/finance/privacy), and documented by
[Helius](https://www.helius.dev/docs/privacy/) as Solana Rings - executes
natively on mainnet rather than on a separate chain.

What it conceals depends on how it is configured. The permissionless Default
Ring keeps the asset and the amount private while leaving sender and recipient
public, so it does not by itself conceal the counterparty graph. Concealing the
graph requires a Custom Ring, which the institution adopts or operates and which
can also carry transfer policy, reporting, and auditor visibility.

These are early-stage products, in beta as of August 2026.
[Risk and diligence](/docs/institutional/risk-and-diligence#privacy) carries the
illustrative assessment, including when to re-assess.

**Consider this if** the counterparty graph itself is the sensitive thing, you
can adopt or operate a Custom Ring that conceals it, and your timeline allows
you to move as these products mature.

## Rung 4 - Solana Private Channels

**In development.**

[Private Channels](/docs/tools/private-channels) moves transfers into payment
channels anchored to Solana, giving an institution-operated perimeter around
execution. [Privacy on Solana](/docs/finance/privacy) describes the model and
how it compares with the other two.

The documentation for it in these docs is currently a pointer to the product
page, the repository, and the brochure rather than a technical reference, so
treat what follows as orientation and take architecture questions to the team
behind it.

Institutionally, the relevant property is that activity inside a channel is not
individually published to the public ledger, while the channel itself remains
anchored to Solana. That fits a bilateral or small-group flow that is
high-frequency and confidential, where the parties still want a public network
as the settlement substrate.

**Consider this if** you have a defined set of counterparties, high transfer
frequency between them, and a confidentiality requirement that rungs 2 and 3 do
not meet.

<Callout type="warn">

Settlement topologies built on this - a channel per bank, netting inside the
channel with periodic net settlement to mainnet, and anything resembling
correspondent banking between channels - are **unresolved designs**. Do not
present them to a risk committee as available architecture.
[Settlement topologies](/docs/institutional/architecture/settlement-topologies)
works through these patterns and states the open questions in each.

</Callout>

## Rung 5 - A Solana Permissioned Environment

**By arrangement.**

A Solana Permissioned Environment (SPE) is a Solana Virtual Machine instance run
independently of Solana mainnet-beta, where the operating institution is the
highest authority.

An SPE is not connected to mainnet-beta, so it has none of the interoperability
the public network provides - no shared liquidity, no composability with other
assets, and no existing custodian or venue support.

**Consider it only when a hard requirement needs it**, rather than scoping one
from documentation.

A preference for greater control does not by itself require an SPE: the
token-layer controls in
[choosing controls](/docs/institutional/token-design/choosing-controls) provide
much of it without giving up interoperability.

<Callout type="info">

SPE requirements, current capabilities, and availability are handled directly by
the Solana Foundation.
[Get in touch](https://solana.com/solutions/solana-permissioned-environments) to
discuss whether an SPE fits your requirement.

</Callout>

## What would change the recommendation

- **A venue or custodian you depend on does not support Token-2022 with your
  extensions.** This constrains rung 1 and 2 and is worth confirming before
  anything else - see
  [program and authority design](/docs/institutional/token-design/program-and-authority-design).
- **The Solana Privacy Protocol reaches general availability.** That would move
  rung 3 from in-development to a genuine option for the concealment a Custom
  Ring provides.
- **A supervisor rules that a public network is unacceptable for the asset.**
  That forces rung 5 regardless of the cost.
- **Your requirement turns out to be amount-dependent policy plus
  confidentiality.** No rung resolves that conflict onchain; see
  [privacy and transfer controls](/docs/institutional/compliance-and-privacy/privacy-and-transfer-controls).
