<Callout type="warn" title="Exploratory">

The patterns on this page are **designs under discussion, not specified
architecture**. Private Channels itself is under development, but the
institutional settlement topologies built on top of it - netting, periodic net
settlement, and arrangements between channels - have open questions that nobody
has closed yet. This page exists so you can reason about them and ask informed
questions. Do not present anything here to a risk committee as available
architecture.

</Callout>

## Why a channel changes the settlement question

On mainnet, every transfer is its own settlement. There is no netting because
there is nothing to net - each transfer is final in its own right, at the
commitment level you chose in
[settlement and finality](/docs/institutional/settlement-finality). That is
simple, and it is sufficient for most institutional issuance.

[Private Channels](/docs/tools/private-channels) introduces a different shape:
transfers happen inside a channel anchored to Solana, where they are not
individually published and carry no network fee. Once activity happens inside a
channel rather than transfer-by-transfer on the public ledger, a question arises
that does not exist on mainnet - **what is the relationship between activity
inside the channel and settlement outside it?**

Every pattern below is an answer to that question. They differ in who
participates in a channel, what gets netted, and when value crosses back to
mainnet.

## Example 1 - A channel per counterparty pair

The simplest shape. Two institutions with a high-frequency bilateral flow open a
channel between them. Transfers inside settle between the two parties without a
mainnet transaction, so they are not individually published. The channel anchors
to Solana, and the bilateral position is settled to mainnet on some cadence.

**Where this fits.** A bilateral relationship with enough volume that
per-transfer public settlement is either too slow, too costly, or too revealing,
and where the two parties already have a credit relationship.

**What is reasonably clear.** The confidentiality benefit and the throughput
benefit. Two named parties, a known bilateral exposure, and a settlement cadence
they agree between themselves.

**Open questions.**

- What is the legal characterization of the in-channel position between
  settlements? An obligation, a claim, a custody relationship?
- What happens on default by one party mid-cycle, before a net settlement?
- Which point is the point of finality for accounting purposes - the in-channel
  transfer or the mainnet settlement? See
  [settlement and finality](/docs/institutional/settlement-finality) for why
  this question has a precise answer on mainnet and not yet here.

## Example 2 - A channel per institution

Instead of a channel per pair, each institution operates one channel that its
clients or counterparties join. The institution is the hub; activity among its
participants is internal to the channel.

**Where this fits.** An institution with many counterparties that all face it,
rather than each other - the shape of a bank and its clients.

**Open questions.**

- The hub is a single point of failure and a single point of trust for everyone
  in the channel. What is the recourse for a participant?
- Participation control inside the channel and holder control at the token layer
  are two separate gates. How do they stay consistent? An address removed from
  the token's allowlist is not automatically out of a channel.
- Does the hub's own regulatory status change by operating the channel?

## Example 3 - "Netting" inside a channel

Rather than settling every in-channel transfer, participants accumulate
positions and settle only the net at intervals. This is the pattern that reduces
the number of mainnet settlements.

**What netting buys.** Fewer mainnet settlements, lower cost, and a settlement
cadence matched to an institution's existing treasury operations rather than to
block times.

**Open questions.**

- **What is being netted?** Obligations between participants, or token balances?
  These have different legal characters and different insolvency treatment.
- **Is the netting legally enforceable?** Close-out netting depends on a netting
  agreement and, in most jurisdictions, on a legal opinion for each jurisdiction
  involved. That is a contractual and legal question that no protocol design
  answers.
- **What is the exposure between settlements?** Netting concentrates settlement
  risk into the settlement moment. The intra-cycle exposure has to be measured,
  limited, and probably collateralized.
- **What happens if net settlement fails?** A failed settlement after a cycle of
  netted activity is a materially worse situation than a failed individual
  transfer, because the activity it represents has already been treated as done.

## Example 4 - Net settlement to mainnet

The complement to pattern 3: whatever is netted inside a channel eventually
crosses to mainnet as a settlement transaction against a cash or tokenized
instrument.

This is where channel activity rejoins the material in
[payments and interoperability](/docs/institutional/payments-and-interoperability).
The cash-leg choices there - stablecoin, tokenized deposit, assets, or
off-ledger confirmation - apply unchanged to the net settlement. The
atomic-exchange property described there is a property of a mainnet transaction,
so it is available for the net settlement itself but not for the in-channel
activity that produced it.

**Open questions.**

- Cadence, and who decides it. A cadence that suits one participant's treasury
  operations may not suit another's.
- Whether the net settlement can be made atomic against the cash leg, which
  determines whether either side carries settlement risk at the crossing point.
- How a participant reconciles in-channel activity against a single net mainnet
  settlement, and what evidence trail that leaves for an examiner.

## Example 5 - Between channels

If institutions each operate a channel, moving value between channels resembles
correspondent banking.

**This is unresolved and should be treated as an open research question.** The
questions include who holds the intermediating position, whether mainnet is the
settlement layer between channels or something else is, how liquidity is
provisioned at each hop, and what the failure modes are when a multi-hop
settlement partially completes.

A common public settlement layer already exists underneath every channel, which
is the starting point any design here would build on.

## How to use this page

If a settlement topology is under discussion in your institution:

1. **Establish which pattern is actually being proposed.** The five above have
   different risk profiles and are often conflated in discussion.
2. **Separate the protocol question from the legal one.** What the software does
   is answerable from documentation; what the in-channel position is between
   settlements is not.
3. **Ask what happens on failure** at each stage - a participant default, a
   failed net settlement, a channel becoming unavailable.
4. **Check whether you need any of it.** Institutional issuance may settle fine
   on mainnet, per
   [the deployment decision guide](/docs/institutional/architecture/deployment-decision-guide).
   Channels add complexity. They are worth considering where bilateral volume is
   high and confidentiality is required.

Take specific architecture questions to the Solana Foundation.
