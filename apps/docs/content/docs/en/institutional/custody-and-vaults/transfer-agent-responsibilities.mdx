<Callout type="info" title="Summary">

The register-of-record decision from
[token design decisions](/docs/institutional/token-design) determines the
division of labor. If the mint is the register, the transfer agent's function
moves onchain and their authority becomes a signing key. If an off-chain
register governs, the mint is a mirror and every onchain action needs a
corresponding entry in the governing register.

</Callout>

## The two arrangements

### The mint is the register

The mint is the operative record of holdings, and no separate book competes with
it. Whether that record is the definitive register as a matter of law is a
separate question - see
[settlement and finality](/docs/institutional/settlement-finality).

The transfer agent's traditional function - maintaining the definitive holder
record - is now performed by the token program. What remains for them is
everything that is not the record itself: identity and eligibility, corporate
actions, holder servicing, and statutory reporting. Their authority becomes
operational: a key that can add to the allowlist, or a co-signature on mint and
burn.

### An off-chain register governs

The book of record sits where it always has and the mint mirrors it.

Every onchain action must have a corresponding entry in the governing register,
and the two must be reconciled continuously rather than periodically. The
transfer agent keeps their existing function unchanged and gains an obligation:
ensuring the mirror does not drift.

Much current issuance is here.

## Dividing the duties

The split below is a starting point. Responsibilities may vary by institution.

| Duty                                     | Typically the issuer          | Typically the transfer agent               |
| ---------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Deciding to issue or redeem              | Yes                           | Executes                                   |
| Holding the mint authority               | Yes, usually a multisig       | Sometimes a co-signer                      |
| Holder eligibility and KYC               | Sets the policy               | Runs the checks                            |
| Maintaining the allowlist                | Owns the gate                 | Usually operates it day to day             |
| Signing mint and burn                    | Yes                           | Co-signs where the arrangement requires it |
| Cap-table maintenance                    | -                             | Yes                                        |
| Reconciling onchain against the register | Shares                        | Shares                                     |
| Corporate actions                        | Declares                      | Executes                                   |
| Holder servicing and statements          | -                             | Yes                                        |
| Exercising the permanent delegate        | Yes, under a governed process | Requests, does not hold                    |
| Statutory and regulatory reporting       | Yes                           | Supports                                   |

One row deserves emphasis.

**Allowlist operation maps onto the transfer agent's existing function.** It is
holder eligibility expressed as an onchain list. Where the eligibility decision
is produced by a check they already run, wiring it through an attestation keeps
the evidence off-chain - see
[attestation-based compliance](/docs/institutional/compliance-and-privacy/attestation-based-compliance).

## Reconciliation

What to compare:

- **Total supply** onchain against the register's total issued. This catches
  mint and burn errors immediately and is cheap to run.
- **Per-holder balances** against the register's positions, keyed by the address
  the register records for each holder.
- **The allowlist** against the register's eligible-holder list. Drift here may
  be an early sign that an onboarding or offboarding process has broken.
- **Frozen accounts** against the register's restricted positions.

Practical points worth settling:

- **Reconcile at a stated commitment level, not "now."** A read at default
  commitment can be superseded. Use finalized for anything that feeds a book of
  record - see
  [settlement and finality](/docs/institutional/settlement-finality).
- **A permanent-delegate transfer moves a balance without holder instruction.**
  Your reconciliation should recognize it as an authorized issuer action rather
  than a break.
- **Scaled UI amount changes the displayed balance without a transfer.** If your
  token uses it, reconcile raw amounts and apply the multiplier for
  presentation, or every rebase becomes a reconciliation exception. See
  [operate the token](/docs/institutional/tutorials/issuing/operate-the-token).
- **An address is not a holder.** One holder may control several addresses, and
  a custodian may hold for many. Your register maps holders to addresses, and
  that mapping is yours to maintain - nothing onchain provides it.

## What to settle before launch

- Which arrangement you are in: onchain register, or mirror.
- Every row of the duty table, explicitly assigned.
- Which authorities the transfer agent holds, and which they may only request.
- Reconciliation frequency, the commitment level used, and who investigates a
  break.
- The holder-to-address mapping's owner and its update process.
