<Callout type="info" title="Summary">

Custody support is an important dependency. Confirm that your custodian holds
Token-2022 with the specific extensions you intend to enable, and that they can
be onboarded to your allowlist, before you finalize the token design. The
failure mode is discovering after launch that your asset cannot be held by the
institutions your holders use.

</Callout>

## Why this comes before the token design is final

A gated Token-2022 asset makes demands that an ordinary token does not. The
custodian's system has to create a token account that starts frozen, get that
account thawed against your gate, and keep working when you exercise controls
they do not control - a pause, a freeze, a forced transfer.

Support is uneven, and it is specific to extensions rather than to Token-2022 as
a whole. A custodian can hold a plain Token-2022 mint and still have no path for
one behind Token ACL.

This is why
[program and authority design](/docs/institutional/token-design/program-and-authority-design)
puts confirmation of extension support ahead of the rest of the build.

## The custody models

| Model                     | Who holds the keys                                  | Where it fits                                                                 |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Qualified custodian**   | A regulated third party, on the holder's behalf     | Where a regulatory obligation requires it, or holders will not self-custody   |
| **Self-custody with HSM** | The institution, in its own hardware                | Treasury and issuer-side keys - your mint and permanent delegate authorities  |
| **MPC / threshold**       | Split across parties, no single complete key exists | Where operational control must be shared without a single point of compromise |
| **Exchange-held**         | A venue, as an operational balance                  | Trading balances, not long-term holdings                                      |

These are not exclusive, and an issuance commonly uses more than one - a
qualified custodian for holders, an HSM or MPC arrangement for the issuer's own
authorities. For the issuer-side keys, the mechanics of each backend are covered
in [choosing a backend](/docs/tools/keychain/choosing-a-backend) and
[production best practices](/docs/tools/keychain/production-best-practices);
this page is about the holder side.

## What to confirm with a custodian

Scope these before you commit to a token design.

**Asset support**

- Token-2022 mints, and specifically each extension you will enable. Ask
  extension by extension rather than accepting "we support Token-2022."
- Accounts that are frozen on creation. Some systems treat a frozen account as
  an error state and will not create or display one.
- Confidential balances, if you are enabling them. This is a substantially
  larger integration than the other extensions - see
  [privacy and transfer controls](/docs/institutional/compliance-and-privacy/privacy-and-transfer-controls).

**Onboarding to the gate**

- Which address the custodian will hold the asset at, and whether it is stable
  or rotates. An allowlist entry is per-address, so a custodian that rotates
  deposit addresses needs a different arrangement from one that does not.
- Whether they can supply that address ahead of the first transfer, so it can be
  allowlisted before any mint is attempted.
- Who at the custodian requests allowlisting, and your SLA for acting on it.

**Living with your controls**

- Behavior when an account they hold is frozen by you. Do they detect it, alert,
  and can they still report the balance?
- Behavior under a token-wide pause.
- Behavior when a permanent delegate moves a balance out of an account they
  hold. This one is easy to overlook: from the custodian's perspective an asset
  left without their instruction, so their reconciliation process should not
  treat it as a loss or an error.

**Reporting**

- Whether they can report holdings at a specific slot or commitment level rather
  than "now." This matters for
  [reconciliation](/docs/institutional/custody-and-vaults/transfer-agent-responsibilities)
  against your register of record.

## What the issuer has to provide

Custody integration is bilateral. The custodian needs things from you:

- **An allowlisting path** with a named contact, a request channel, and a
  committed turnaround. Manual email onboarding does not scale to a production
  holder base.
- **Incident contacts** reachable on your pause authority's timescale. If you
  pause the token, custodians holding it generally expect to hear from you, not
  discover it through their monitoring.
- **Advance notice of authority rotation.** A custodian watching your
  authorities as a control may treat an unannounced rotation as a red flag.
- **Documentation of what each control does**, in terms their risk function can
  assess. For example: "permanent delegate" is a forced-transfer power - state
  when it would be used and what governs its use.

## Decide these before you build

- Which custody model each class of holder will use.
- Extension-by-extension support confirmation from each custodian.
- Address stability, and how allowlisting works for rotating addresses.
- Your allowlisting SLA and the contact on both sides.
- Custodian behavior under freeze, pause, and forced transfer.
