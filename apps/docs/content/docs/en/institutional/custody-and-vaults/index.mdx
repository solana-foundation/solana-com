<Callout type="info" title="Summary">

Everything in this section is about a third party holding your asset. A gated
Token-2022 mint makes demands an ordinary token does not, and each holder -
custodian, transfer agent, vault program - has to be able to meet them.
Confirming that comes before the token design is final, not after launch.

</Callout>

## The three holders

**A custodian** holds the asset for someone else. Their systems have to create a
token account that starts frozen, get it thawed against your gate, and keep
working when you exercise a control they do not control. Support is uneven and
specific to extensions rather than to Token-2022 as a whole.

**A transfer agent** maintains the holder record. Where the mint is the register
of record their traditional function moves onchain and their authority becomes a
signing key; where an off-chain register governs, they keep their existing
function and gain an obligation to keep the mirror from drifting.

**A vault** is a program that holds assets and issues claims against them. It is
subject to your gate like any other holder - the gate does not exempt programs -
and the accounts it touches have to be allowlisted before the first deposit.

## What they have in common

Each one needs the same thing from you: a way onto the allowlist with a named
contact and a committed turnaround, notice before an authority rotates, and
documentation of what each control does in terms their risk function can assess.
A control you can exercise unilaterally is a control someone else has to be able
to detect and explain.
