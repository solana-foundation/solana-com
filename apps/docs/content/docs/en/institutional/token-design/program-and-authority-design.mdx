<Callout type="info" title="Summary">

Issue on Token-2022 unless you have a named requirement it cannot meet. Give
every authority its own key from the start, and decide for each one whether it
should be a program-enforced PDA, an onchain multisig, or a single custodied
key. Every program in your token's transfer path - including a transfer hook you
write - forms part of the asset's dependency surface.

</Callout>

## Which token program

Three options, in the order you should consider them.

| Option                     | What you get                                                                     | What it costs                                                                                                                                                |
| -------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **SPL Token**              | The original token program. Universal support everywhere on Solana               | No issuer control surface beyond freeze. No default-frozen accounts, pause, or permanent delegate                                                            |
| **Token-2022**             | The same core behavior plus the extension set that regulated issuance depends on | Support is broad but not universal. Confirm your venues, custodians, and wallets handle it                                                                   |
| **A custom token program** | Anything you can write                                                           | You own the audit, the upgrade authority, the liveness, and the integration burden. No wallet, indexer, custodian, or venue supports it without bespoke work |

**SPL Token is generally not enough for a supervised issuance.** It has a freeze
authority and nothing else - no way to make accounts frozen on creation, no
pause, no permanent delegate, and so no forced transfer for a court order. If
your token needs none of those, you likely do not need this section.

**Token-2022 is the default.** The controls a regulated issuer reaches for are
extensions enforced inside the program itself, so they are shared standards
rather than code you maintain. See [Token Extensions](/docs/tokens/extensions)
for the full surface and
[choosing controls](/docs/institutional/token-design/choosing-controls) for
which ones to turn on.

The constraint with Token-2022 is usually reach rather than capability. Before
you commit, confirm that your custodian, your transfer agent, the venues you
expect to list on, and any wallet your holders will use support Token-2022 mints
**with the specific extensions you intend to enable**. Support for the program
does not imply support for every extension on it - a venue may handle a plain
Token-2022 mint and still reject one carrying a transfer hook.

**A custom program is a last resort.** Writing your own means you own an audit
before launch and after every change, an upgrade authority that becomes its own
governance question, and the fact that no wallet, indexer, custodian, or venue
will understand your token without bespoke work. Reach for it only when you can
name the requirement that Token-2022 plus a transfer hook cannot express, and
record that requirement.

## Authority design

Token-2022 lets every power sit on a different key.
[Choosing controls](/docs/institutional/token-design/choosing-controls#split-authorities-from-the-start)
lists the authorities and who should hold each. This section is about what
_kind_ of holder each one should be.

There are three shapes, and the choice is a trade between how fast an authority
can act and how hard it is to misuse.

### A single custodied key

One keypair, held in an HSM or a custody service. Fastest to act, simplest to
operate, and a single point of compromise.

Appropriate for low-consequence, high-frequency authorities - the metadata
authority is the clearest example. Also the pragmatic choice for the **pause
authority**, which is only useful if it can be exercised within minutes of an
incident. A pause that requires multiple signatures out of hours may not be
available when the incident occurs.

### An onchain multisig

M-of-N approval enforced by the token program, so no single holder can act
alone. Slower, auditable, and the right default for anything whose misuse would
be hard to reverse.

Use it for the **mint authority** (new supply), the **permanent delegate**
(moving or burning a holder's balance), and the **Confidential Balances
authority**. For the permanent delegate in particular, the slowness is
deliberate: its legitimate uses are court orders, fraud response, and lost-key
recovery, all of which you should be prepared to evidence afterwards. Court
orders and lost-key recovery normally allow time to convene a quorum. Active
fraud may not, so decide in advance whether the pause authority covers that gap
while the delegate quorum assembles.

See [adding signers](/docs/tools/keychain/adding-signers) for the mechanics and
[production best practices](/docs/tools/keychain/production-best-practices) for
how to hold the underlying keys. Do not restate key management here - decide the
shape, then follow those pages.

### A PDA

A program-derived address has no private key. It can only act when a program
executes a code path that signs for it, so the policy is enforced by code rather
than by whoever holds a key.

This is the right shape when the rule is mechanical and you want to remove human
discretion from it entirely. The clearest case is the **freeze authority under
Token ACL**: delegating it to the ACL program's PDA turns "the issuer thaws each
account by hand" into "any address that satisfies the gate thaws itself," which
removes the issuer as a liveness dependency for every new holder.

The trade is that a PDA can only ever do what the program allows. If the program
has no code path for the action you need, the authority cannot take it. So
retain an issuer override wherever the law might require you to act outside the
policy - with Token ACL, the ability to freeze or thaw directly, swap the gate,
or reclaim the freeze authority.

<Callout type="warn">

Whatever shape you choose, plan rotation before you mint. Setting an authority
at creation is straightforward; rotating one afterwards is a separate procedure
per authority type, and a multisig or PDA rotation is not the same operation as
a single-key rotation. Rehearse each on devnet before you need it on mainnet.

</Callout>

## Audit and upgrade risk

Every program that can execute in your token's transfer path is a dependency
your reviewers may treat as part of the asset. For a Token-2022 mint with
frozen-by-default accounts and Token ACL, that list is at least: the Token-2022
program, the Token ACL program, and the gate program the ACL delegates to. Add a
transfer hook and it is your program too.

For each one, be able to answer:

- **Who holds the upgrade authority?** A program whose upgrade authority is a
  single key held by a third party is a party that can change your token's
  behavior without your consent.
- **Is it immutable, and should it be?** Revoking the upgrade authority removes
  that risk and removes your ability to fix a bug. For a program you wrote and
  audited against a fixed spec, immutability is often right. For one still
  tracking a draft standard, it is not.
- **When was it last audited, and against which version?** An audit covers a
  commit, not a program. If the deployed program has been upgraded since, the
  audit is evidence about different code.
- **What happens if it stops working?** A gate program that becomes unavailable
  does not freeze existing balances, but it can stop new holders from
  onboarding. Know which of your operations degrade and which stop.

<Callout type="warn">

Token ACL is specified in sRFC-37, which is not finalized. The standard may
change before it settles, so treat the ACL and gate programs as dependencies
with their own deployment, upgrade, audit, and availability characteristics,
distinct from your mint. See [Token ACL](/docs/tokenization/token-acl) for the
current state.

</Callout>

If you write a transfer hook, you inherit all four questions for your own
program, plus the obligation to keep it working. A hook that panics makes every
transfer of your token fail.

## Decide these before you build

- Which token program, and - if Token-2022 - confirmation of extension support
  from your custodian, transfer agent, and venues.
- The shape of every authority: PDA, multisig, or single key, and where the keys
  live.
- The rotation procedure for each authority, rehearsed on devnet.
- The full list of programs in your transfer path, with the upgrade authority
  and audit status of each.
