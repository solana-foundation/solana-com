# Breakpoint Bound: Getting Around London, Finding Scran, and Some Sights to See (Part 3)

Part 3/5 in the @SolanaEvents guide to thriving (and surviving) at Breakpoint London 2026

Your flights are booked, room locked in, entry requirements sorted, and bags are packed; Article 2 cleared that runway. Now let’s tackle getting from arrivals to the venue floor, finding solid places for scran (food) between the conference and side events, and navigating the city without wasting hours deciphering routes or sitting in transit.

Consider this your introductory playbook for everything between the hotel lobby and [Olympia](https://www.olympia.co.uk/getting-here) (the main venue for Breakpoint 2026), along with a few solid reasons to explore further. We’re not in the tour guide business, so this isn’t a sightseeing list; it’s on-the-ground intelligence so your time in London for Breakpoint also feels like you visited London.

### Getting In: Heathrow + Gatwick

Most likely you’re flying into Heathrow or Gatwick. Either way, compare the whole journey to your hotel, not just the airport train: changes, walking, luggage, all of it.

The fastest train on paper isn't automatically your best route.

#### From Heathrow

[Elizabeth line](https://www.heathrow.com/transport-and-directions/by-rail-or-train/elizabeth-line): The balanced pick for heading into central London and beyond, with services through to east London. The Lizzy line deserves its praise, but check whether it takes you toward your hotel or past it.

[Heathrow Express](https://www.heathrowexpress.com/journey-planning/heathrow-london): The Paddington speedrun: about 15 minutes from Heathrow Terminals 2 & 3, with extra time from Terminal 5. Compare the fare and onward journey before paying for speed you might lose on the next connection.

[Piccadilly line](https://www.heathrow.com/transport-and-directions/by-rail-or-train/london-underground): Heathrow describes Piccadilly as its most cost-effective rail route into London. If you're based around Hammersmith or Earl’s Court, check this before defaulting to a trip through Paddington.

Black cab or rideshare: worth comparing if you're exhausted, carrying a lot, need accessible transport, or can split the ride. Just don't assume door-to-door means quicker. Check the live journey time as well as the quote.

If you want to do this on a budget, then get the Piccadilly line two stops to Hounslow West and then Uber from there. It will save you the airport surcharge (takes a £40 fare to £15).

#### From Gatwick

[Gatwick Express](https://www.gatwickexpress.com/about-us): Non-stop to [Victoria](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=London%20Victoria%20station) in about 30 minutes, then take the District line to West London

[Southern](https://www.southernrailway.com/journey/gatwick-airport-to-london-victoria): Serves Victoria and [Clapham Junction](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Clapham%20Junction%20station), making it worth checking for a west London destination.

[Thameslink](https://www.thameslinkrailway.com/travel-information/airport-travel/gatwick-airport): Serves [London Bridge](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=London%20Bridge%20station), [Blackfriars](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=London%20Blackfriars%20station), [Farringdon](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Farringdon%20station%20London), and [St Pancras](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=St%20Pancras%20International%20station%20London), among other stops; compare it if your base is farther east or north.

### Beating the Jet Lag

You may not have two spare days to feel human again. Front-load your schedule lightly, especially if you're landing straight into a week of sessions and side events.

Shift before you fly\! Nudge bedtime and wake-up time toward London time before departure; small adjustments beat arriving completely out of sync.

Hydrate like it's your job. Drink water, move around during the flight, and go easy on alcohol and caffeine. The welcome drinks can wait.

Get outside during the day. Natural light helps your body clock adjust, and switching to local sleeping hours helps too, obviously. Drop your bags, then try a jaunt around the neighborhood rather than immediately disappearing under the duvet.

Give yourself permission to skip the first late-night thing. Showing up functional beats showing up everywhere.

### Use the Tube. Uber Is the Backup Plan.

For this trip, make the London Tube your first route check. Don't reflexively order rideshare for every coffee, dinner, or side event, especially when you're moving across town. Save rideshare for luggage, accessibility needs, or a late connection (if you're out past 1am, Uber home and skip the night buses). Less time in transit means more of your Breakpoint.

#### Tap, ride, repeat

Paying as you go with contactless keeps everyday travel simple, with [daily caps](https://tfl.gov.uk/fares/find-fares/capping) based on your journeys and zones; the travel day runs from 4:30am to 4:29am the next morning. Most lines stop around midnight, with Night Tube on some lines on Fridays and Saturdays. You don't need to become a fare-system scholar, but these rules matter:

Use the same card or device throughout. Your physical card, phone, and watch count separately, even when they’re linked to the same bank account. Alternatively, you can get an [Oyster](https://oyster.tfl.gov.uk/oyster/entry.do) card.

Tap in AND out on rail journeys. On buses, tap in only; missed rail taps can trigger maximum fares.

Don't assume one cap covers everything. Non-tube lines like Heathrow Express and Gatwick Express journeys [don't count toward TfL's daily or weekly caps](https://content.tfl.gov.uk/tfl-conditions-of-carriage.pdf). Check airport fares separately\!

#### A few habits that make life easier

- Check the live route. Use [Google Maps](https://www.google.com/maps), [Citymapper](https://citymapper.com/london), or Transport for London ([TfL](https://tfl.gov.uk/)) before leaving and check the last train before committing to “one more” at the mixer or pub. Breakpoint runs Sunday–Tuesday, so don't bank on the [Friday/Saturday Night Tube](https://tfl.gov.uk/modes/tube/night-tube).

- Dodge the busiest windows when you can. As a planning rule of thumb, try to avoid major central interchanges around 7–9am and 4:30–6:30pm on weekdays. Give yourself breathing room rather than scheduling a cross-city sprint.

- Compare the walk or a Lime bike. Sometimes the best connection is your feet or the pedals, not a ride with two changes. Between central London venues, a bike is often fastest: London Hacker House to Soho is 10–15 mins by bike and 20+ on the Tube.

- Be easy to share a train with. Let people off first, move away from the doors, and get your backpack out of somebody else's face.

### Know Your Lines, Not Just Your Neighborhood

“Head east” isn't a route. Pin the actual venue, check your nearest station, and look at the number of changes before deciding something is convenient.

#### Olympia: the station is right there

[Kensington (Olympia) station](https://www.olympia.co.uk/getting-here) is beside the venue, served by the Overground's [Mildmay line](https://tfl.gov.uk/modes/london-overground/) and Southern rail services. These are the useful options:

- Mildmay line, light blue: Use the Clapham Junction branch for Kensington (Olympia), rather than assuming every Mildmay train goes there.

- District line, green: West Kensington or Baron’s Court plus a walk is a practical approach; direct District trains to Kensington (Olympia) are limited, mainly running at weekends, with extra weekday service for some major events.

- Piccadilly line, dark blue: Hammersmith is another approach, followed by a walk or bus to the venue. Check live routes and station works before traveling.

→ A familiar line name isn't enough. Check the destination on the train.

### Beyond Olympia: Where to Eat, Drink + Get Some London XP

Article 2 covered where to sleep. Now work outward from Olympia: stay nearby for a quick reset, head toward central London for a proper afternoon out, or make Soho the evening plan. These are options, not a checklist. Nobody needs sightseeing FOMO on top of conference FOMO.

- [Kensington](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Kensington%20London) / [Holland Park](https://www.rbkc.gov.uk/parks-leisure-and-culture/parks/your-local-park/holland-park): a breather beyond the hall. Check out design and architecture exhibits at the [Design Museum](https://designmuseum.org/plan-your-visit) on [Kensington High Street](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Kensington%20High%20Street%20London), or duck into Holland Park to clear your head in [Kyoto Garden](https://www.rbkc.gov.uk/parks-leisure-and-culture/parks/your-local-park/holland-park). Make the park a daylight plan; gates close around dusk. When hunger hits, the [Churchill Arms](https://www.churchillarmskensington.co.uk/) serves up solid Thai dishes inside a classic flower-filled pub.

- [Hammersmith](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Hammersmith%20London) / [Shepherd's Bush](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Shepherds%20Bush%20London): pubs, gigs, practical errands. [The Dove](https://www.dovehammersmith.co.uk/) in Hammersmith has cozy rooms, cask beer, and Thames views. For entertainment, check the [Eventim Apollo](https://www.eventimapollo.com/) or [O2 Shepherd's Bush Empire](https://www.academymusicgroup.com/o2shepherdsbushempire); [Westfield London](https://www.westfield.com/en/united-kingdom/london) covers shopping and food when the group can't agree. Pick the pub for a small catch-up, the gig for a night off from talking shop.

- [South Kensington](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=South%20Kensington%20London): culture, then something good to eat. The [V&A](https://www.vam.ac.uk/south-kensington) is the art, design, and performance stop; or explore the [Natural History Museum](https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=%2Fm%2F01t372) [or](https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=%2Fm%2F01t372) [Science Museum](https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=%2Fm%2F013yrz). Nearby, [CERU](https://cerurestaurants.com/south-kensington/) serves Eastern Mediterranean food just off [Old Brompton Road](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Old%20Brompton%20Road%20London). Give this a real lunch break or spare afternoon, rather than speedrunning a museum between calls. It also works when the weather kills the park plan.

- [Chelsea](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Chelsea%20London) / [King's Road](https://www.kingsroad.co.uk/): the polished afternoon-to-evening option. Think boutiques along King's Road, contemporary art at the [Saatchi Gallery](https://www.saatchigallery.com/), or a performance at the [Royal Court Theatre](https://royalcourttheatre.com/visit/). For dinner, the [Cadogan Arms](https://thecadoganarms.london/) on King's Road does elevated pub classics, including fish and chips, pies, and steaks. Check the menu and book for a group.

- [Soho](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Soho%20London) / [Chinatown](https://chinatown.co.uk/en/) / [Covent Garden](https://www.coventgarden.london/): dinner that turns into a night out. Soho picks include [Kiln](https://kilnsoho.com/) for Thai grills and claypots and [Bubala](https://bubala.co.uk/restaurant/soho/) for meat-free Middle Eastern small plates. [Bar Termini](https://bar-termini-soho.com/) on [Old Compton Street](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Old%20Compton%20Street%20Soho%20London) does coffee and Italian aperitivo in a tiny space: think two-person catch-up, not the entire TG group chat. Chinatown and Covent Garden are within walking distance, with West End entertainment in the mix. Kiln currently takes [walk-ins only](https://kilnsoho.com/reservations/), so build in a wait. Pick dinner first, then decide how ambitious the evening gets.

Late night adventurers

In London, most places close at midnight, and there are only a few 24/7 spots. Try Hippodrome Casino in Soho/Trafalgar Square, or if you’re in East London, a late-night stop at Polo Bar, Duck & Waffle, Sushi Samba.

Plus for those lucky enough to get a pass, the Superteam UK office at Somerset House is 24/7.

#### A little farther out from Olympia, if you have the time

[South Bank](https://southbank.london/): A riverside walk for [London Eye](https://www.londoneye.com/) and [Big Ben](https://www.parliament.uk/bigben/) views, with the [Southbank Centre](https://www.southbankcentre.co.uk/) and [National Theatre](https://www.nationaltheatre.org.uk/) along the way. Your “Proof of London” photo, handled.

[Shoreditch](https://www.visitlondon.com/things-to-do/london-areas/hoxton-and-shoreditch) / [Spitalfields](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Spitalfields%20London) / [Brick Lane](https://www.visitlondon.com/things-to-do/place/279336-brick-lane-market): Street art, market browsing, and food around [Old Spitalfields Market](https://oldspitalfieldsmarket.com/) and Brick Lane, well known for its curry. Make east London a deliberate outing, not a detour between Olympia sessions.

[The British Museum](https://www.britishmuseum.org/) is one of the best in the world, with a collection of 8 million objects spanning human history (plus admission is free\!)

If you’re staying in London for more than a few days, there are football games the week before (Arsenal v Hull), Shakespeare at the Globe, shows at the West End, and other concerts & activities to explore.

### Money in Your Pocket + AWARENESS

A few reminders:

1. Check your card setup before travel. Review overseas fees and any bank travel settings, and keep a backup payment method separate.

1. Most restaurants already include a service charge on your bill; otherwise [10–15% is customary but discretionary](https://www.visitlondon.com/traveller-information/essential-information/money/tipping), while drinks at a pub generally don't call for a tip.

1. Keep your phone close. Don't leave it on an outdoor table or stand distracted at the curb with it held out. It is your wallet, your ticket, and possibly your hotel key this week.

1. [Know the emergency numbers.](https://www.gov.uk/contact-police) Call 999 for an emergency or immediate danger, 111 for non-emergency medical matters, and 101 for non-emergency police matters.

Charge the portable battery. Save your hotel address. Stay aware without spending the whole week looking over your shoulder.
