# Day 1 at Breakpoint 2026: Mainnet 1.0, Zelle, and London’s Crypto Moment

Weeks of UKmaxxing, tips on tube travel and finding the true essence of “the Londoner” have led to this moment: Breakpoint 2026. More than 6,000 community members and 250 institutions flocked to Solana’s “finance fantasy world” at Olympia London.

Walking in you could already feel the excitement. Attendees flocked to sponsor booths to get the latest merch, while the main stage packed out to hear the opening address.

## Day 1 Highlights

Here’s a quick rundown of what was announced on the main stage:

- Solana Foundation President Lily Liu and Chief Strategy Officer Rachel Conlan announced [Solana Mainnet 1.0](https://x.com/solana/status/2122273471497425282), cutting block times from 400ms to 200ms and finality from 13 seconds to 150 milliseconds.

- Zelle completed its first live confidential transfer on Solana, sending $1.25 million in ZelleUSD (ZLUSD) on stage, and confirmed ZelleUSD will launch on Solana for international payments.

- MoneyGram announced a partnership with Solflare that lets eligible users convert USDC to cash at MoneyGram locations in 170 countries and territories.

- Bitso announced that MXNB, its Mexican peso stablecoin, is now live on Solana.

- Ledger launched Clear Signing for Solana and showcased Ledger Atom, which will ship with Solana Mobile’s Seeker app and the Solana dApp Store preinstalled.

- Baillie Gifford and BNY shared the story behind [the UK’s first natively tokenised fund on Solana](https://x.com/solana/status/2069012917484400991).

- ICE’s custody arm featured a [new partnership with Abra](https://x.com/AbraGlobal/status/2122357674725761213).

This year’s Breakpoint focused on the “Token Supercycle,” tracking the movement of economic activity onchain from the heart of one of the world’s historic financial centres. Bringing together financial institutions, technology companies, policymakers and investors, the conversations on Day 1 ranged from stablecoins to tokenised stocks to government policy and Wall Street adoption.

“This is not a price cycle, it's a structural shift where money, assets and ownership are all moving to always-on internet infrastructure,” said Solana Foundation President Lily Liu. “The last digital era was built on software and cheap money. The next one, which we're in the midst of, is being built from steel, silicon and energy. And that takes serious amounts of capital. Capital has to move as fast as the things that are being built. And tokenization is what lets that happen.”

Lily was joined onstage by Solana Foundation Chief Strategy Officer Rachel Conlan, who described Breakpoint as the place where institutions and enterprises share what they’re bringing onchain. “That's something that we really want to ensure that we are bringing those conversations together and setting the agenda for what’s next,” she said.

With that, the two made the first groundbreaking announcement of the day. After six years in beta, Solana has moved to Mainnet 1.0. Now live, the upgrade cuts block times from 400ms to 200ms and finality from 13 seconds to 150 milliseconds.

“With Solana, you have infrastructure which is finally fast enough, cheap enough, performant enough for real economic activity,” said Lily.

## Flattening Finance

A film set the tone of the day with the voices of freelancers waiting weeks on invoices and families losing a slice of every remittance to fees. "By the time my money gets home, everyone's taking little pieces of it. How is that fair?" one voice asked.

Lily's answer was that the barrier is technical rather than geographic. "Tokenization expands access, it expands distribution. It makes every asset equally discoverable and accessible from anywhere," she said. "So you don't need to be on the right exchange or the right index to have a chance at attracting capital. So this geography problem with capital markets is actually a technology problem. A technology problem that internet capital markets solve and Solana is solving it."

Solana Foundation Head of Payments Growth Amira Valliani highlighted the issues in traditional finance that the Foundation wants to address over the next two days of programming, which she billed as a tour of asset ownership. Some 1.3 billion adults worldwide still have no bank or mobile money account, and 41% of adults hold less than $10,000 in wealth. Closer to home, 80% of UK households hold no stocks or investment assets.

"Today is about boomer assets, tomorrow is about zoomer assets," she told the room. "Let's have the hard questions in this room so we can build for the next two years." Day 1 was billed as institutional day, but the thread running through every session was how to make those institutions work for the average person.

## Making Waves in Global Remittances

"Sending money around the world is a pain, it's complex, it's fragmented and it's often unfamiliar," said Zelle VP of Digital Asset Partnerships and Strategy Alexandra Lang. "Over half of the remittance services that are offered today don't deliver money as fast as we'd like them to."

Within the first hour of the event, Solana's place in fixing that was made clear by MoneyGram CMO Lamia Pardo and Zelle’s Alexandra Lang. Both took to the main stage to announce major Solana integrations.

First up was Pardo, who [announced](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/solflare-and-moneygram-ramps-offer-crypto-to-cash-in-170-countries-and-territories-302940133.html) a partnership between the remittance giant and Solflare, building on the MoneyGram and Solana partnership unveiled earlier this year. "As we build the future of digital money, we constantly challenge ourselves to think about every person in the ecosystem, that every single person in the world has the option to move, spend and access the money in the way that makes the most sense," she said. From today, eligible Solflare users can convert USDC into cash at MoneyGram locations in 170 countries and territories, with zero fees on those withdrawals until the end of the year.

In the next session, Zelle previewed its work on Solana. Owned by seven of the largest US banks and with more than 2,400 banks and credit unions, Zelle processed over $1 trillion in payments last year. It is now building ZelleUSD, a dollar-backed stablecoin developed with Anchorage Digital, on Solana and will work with Solana developers to test confidential transfers and balances. The goal is to protect customers' sensitive payment data while keeping the fraud and compliance checks banks need to work with institutions around the world.

Several speakers also made the case that local currencies need their own onchain rails.

"A peso problem can't be solved with the dollar," said Ben Reid, who leads stablecoins and strategic initiatives at Latin American digital asset platform Bitso. He added that tokenised equities and real-world assets that used stablecoins backed with local currencies could solve issues with banking access in nations all over the world, before announcing that MXNB, the Mexican peso stablecoin issued by Bitso, is now live on Solana.

"The dollar may be the first place that proves that onchain markets can solve challenges like this. It won't be the only place that it matters," Reid said.

## Security at the Moment of Signing

Ledger Chief Human Agency Officer Ian Rogers took the main stage to announce Clear Signing for Solana, which shows users in readable terms what they are approving on their device.

"We've been working on it for years," Rogers said after his session. "With Ledger you have security at rest. But if you can't read what it is you're signing, then you don't have transaction time security. That's a big hole to plug."

Rogers also showcased Ledger Atom, an Android-based device first unveiled in Paris in October that puts Ledger's secure element inside a phone-like touchscreen. When it switches into secure mode, a dedicated chip cuts the connection to the rest of the operating system, "so it's literally just like you're using a Ledger," he said. Every Atom will ship with Solana Mobile's Seeker app and the Solana dApp Store preinstalled, after [Solana Mobile named Ledger its first hardware expansion partner.](https://solanamobile.com/blog/solana-mobile-announces-ledger-as-its-first-hardware-expansion-partner) That makes Atom the third mobile device in the Solana ecosystem after Saga and Seeker, and the first built outside Solana Mobile. "If you're a developer on Solana, that means you just have more distribution," Rogers said.

## London Calling

The choice of host city was part of the core message of the day. "London is really one of the homes of capital. It is a very special place. It is ultimately where the clocks are set," Rachel said earlier in the day. "It's where global markets are traded. It is ultimately where financial common law is created."

That pitch got an official seal from Howard Dawber, Deputy Mayor of London for Business and Growth. Dawber told the room that 60 to 70 crypto businesses have signed up to enter the UK's new regulatory framework, calling it "a coming of age moment" for crypto in the country.

The UKmaxxing theme carried into the afternoon as a flash mob brass band dressed in Union Jacks ushered in the headline debate on why crypto is key to UK economic growth. Former Prime Minister Boris Johnson and Lord Ross Kempsell took the stage with The Block's head of multimedia, Gareth Jenkinson, moderating.

## Bringing Institutions Onchain

If the morning was about opening finance up to everyone, the afternoon belonged to the institutions that have spent decades running it. Asset managers and exchange operators explained why they’re building onchain, and their message was consistent: traditional and onchain finance are converging.

Baillie Gifford and BNY set the tone with the story of how an Edinburgh fund house and its long-standing banking partner came together to launch the UK’s first natively tokenised fund on Solana. “I think of capital, it’s a bit like water. It’s always going to go to the lowest resistance point,” said Katherine Hamilton, Partner, Digital Assets at Bailie Gifford, who called the idea of two permanently separate financial systems “just crazy.” Patrick Corker, Managing Director, Market Transformation & Innovation at BNY, was just as firm. “We are committed to this space. Every single day, we’re building in this space,” he said.

Operators of some of the world’s biggest markets are paying close attention too. “Traditional finance can learn a lot, I think, from what is going on in the new areas of finance that leverage blockchain,” said Hester Serafini of ICE, whose custody arm also featured in a new partnership with Abra announced from the same stage.

The final panel turned to US policy. Patrick Witt, former executive director of the President’s Council for Digital Assets at the White House, joined Anna Irrera, Bloomberg senior editor, to discuss where US crypto policy goes after the midterms. Witt spent more than a year negotiating the Clarity Act, and while it did not pass, he attributed it to timing and the politics of an election year, saying the policy differences had largely been resolved.

Even so, he left the stage optimistic. He expects US market structure to be shaped less on Capitol Hill and more through regulators’ own rulemaking, and he sees a younger generation of lawmakers on both sides treating crypto as part of the financial system. He also opened a door for the UK, describing the stalled bill as a chance for markets elsewhere to step up and win share.

“If you’re America and you want to make sure that you are remaining the global capital market center of the world, you have to be taking actions to make sure that your markets are staying at the pace of technological progress,” he said. “Capital’s going to go where it’s treated best.”

As the lights came down on the main stage, the MC sent the crowd off to the evening’s side events with a reminder of what brought everyone to London: “Solana’s vision as the base for universal basic ownership of all assets anywhere, any place.” Thank you to everyone who joined us at Olympia for day one. Enjoy the side events, and see you back for day two\!
