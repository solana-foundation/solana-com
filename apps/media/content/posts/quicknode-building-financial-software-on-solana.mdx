**Quicknode has released “Building Financial Software on Solana”, a new book by Mike MacCana that explains how real financial products work as programs on Solana, including exchanges, lending markets, funds and options trading platforms. The full book is free to read at [solanabook.org](https://www.solanabook.org/), and a paperback is available on [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0HMJSSB95) and [Barnes & Noble](https://www.barnesandnoble.com/w/building-financial-software-on-solana-mike-maccana/1151680819).**

![Cover of Building Financial Software on Solana by Mike MacCana](/uploads/posts/quicknode-building-financial-software-on-solana/book-cover.webp)

## What the book covers

Most material about Solana explains how the network works. “Building Financial Software on Solana” is about what you can build on it.

The book runs to more than 300 pages with 115 diagrams. Every account, transaction and movement of money is drawn in one visual language, so readers can see how a program works independently of reading any code.

**Part I** covers the fundamentals: Solana’s accounts, tokens and time, then the arithmetic of money, how real-world prices get onchain safely, and how to defend against the attacks that have drained real markets.

**Part II** builds ten complete, tested programs. Each chapter takes a familiar financial product and shows, step by step, how it runs onchain:

- **Exchanges:** an order book that matches buyers and sellers like NYSE or NASDAQ, a constant-product AMM that prices trades from a pool of tokens, and a prop AMM where a market maker quotes its own capital at oracle prices
- **Lending:** how interest rates are set, how interest builds up every second, and how liquidations protect lenders
- **Funds:** an onchain managed fund, covering how deposits turn into fund shares and how the fund rebalances its holdings
- **Options, perpetual futures and betting markets:** how payoffs, funding rates, margin and pooled bets are calculated and settled
- **Payments, escrow and fundraising:** moving stablecoins such as USDC safely between parties who don’t trust each other, and all-or-nothing crowdfunding

*Pages 138–139, from the order book chapter: how bids and asks sit on either side of the spread, and how the book stores orders so the best price always fills first.*

The examples use familiar assets that readers know and care about, such as USDC and tokenized stocks.

## Written so non-developers can follow along

You don’t need to be an engineer to get value from the book. It is written so that product managers, analysts, founders and finance professionals can follow what each program does: how an instruction is handled, how interest is calculated, and how shares in a liquidity pool are created. Developers can build on every program in the book: the code is open source in the [companion repository](https://github.com/quicknode/solana-program-examples), where it is tested and kept up to date.

## No equivalent for any other chain

There is currently no book, training course or video series that comprehensively shows how financial products work for any other blockchain. For teams deciding where to build financial applications, that gives Solana a head start.

Since its release on October 13, the book has reached **#3 in Amazon’s Hot New Releases for Financial Engineering**, ranking alongside a wave of new AI trading titles.

## About the author

Mike MacCana creates Solana and finance content at Quicknode, where he maintains the most comprehensive open library of tested Solana programs: the companion repository this book is built on. Before Quicknode, he ran developer education at the Solana Foundation, where he created a four-day education program taught at Ivy League universities, and co-created the Solana Foundation Developer Bootcamp, watched over 330,000 times on YouTube. Before the Foundation, he won the RWA and Messaging prizes at the Solana Sandstorm hackathon for Portal Payments.

Before Solana, Mike worked at large financial firms including Man Group, Credit Suisse, and CMC Invest, and at Google and Red Hat. He lives in New York City and is on X as @mikemaccana.
