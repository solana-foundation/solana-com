# Solana Events Article 2: Booked Breakpoint? Let's Get You Prepped

Whether you're a solo builder counting every dollar, a founder flying a team in, or a first-timer who's never done a crypto conference on another continent, the goal is the same: land in London with your flights, hotel, gear, wallet, and schedule locked down, so you can spend your energy on the stuff that matters once you're there.

Flights. Hotels. Layers. eSIM. Wallet hygiene. Let's get it all sorted before you touch English soil.

## BOOK SMART, FRENS

You already know the mantra from Part 1: BOOK EARLY. Here's exactly how to turn that into cheaper flights and a better hotel.

### Flights

- Set fare alerts the day you commit. Google Flights, Skyscanner, and Hopper track a route and ping you when the price drops, set them up the moment you decide you’re going.

- Know your airport. London has six, and they’re not equal. Heathrow (LHR) is the move: most international routes, Elizabeth Line straight into central London. Gatwick (LGW) is a solid second via Gatwick Express, just budget extra time. London City is nice if a route works out; Stansted’s fine but the train into town is \~£20; Southend’s too far; Luton, just don’t.

- General rule of thumb: for most routes, the sweet spot for booking is roughly 6 weeks to 4 months out, earlier for peak/holiday periods. Breakpoint is event-driven travel into a busy November in London, so if you’re reading this the time to book was yesterday.

- Fly in with a buffer. Arriving a few days early at a bare minimum is both cheaper as mid-week fares beat weekend-adjacent ones and smarter for jet lag. Furthermore, if you did want to arrive early, there will be countless events for networking and building starting November 1st, which we covered in the [Article 1 of this series](https://x.com/SolanaEvents/status/2092656457980219570?s=20)\!

### Hotels

London is one of its busiest periods from late November onward, so the good rooms near the venue go fast and only get pricier: book as far ahead as possible, no hesitation. Rooms within a ten-minute walk of Olympia can run well above what you'd pay twenty minutes out once November hits, and the cheap ones vanish first.

A few tactics to consider:

- Start near the venue, then expand your search. Olympia rests along Hammersmith Road in West London, fully reachable via the District Line (Green) and Overground Mildmay Line (Orange). Properties right on top of the conference offer maximum convenience, though they command top dollar and get snagged early. Expanding your perimeter a short distance yields serious value. Filtering along direct Tube lines is smart, given underground transit remains the city's preferred (and recommended) method of transportation.

Heading east opens up endless stays as you approach Soho or Shoreditch, where late-night social spots and official side events naturally gather. Alternatively, spots further out like Richmond (yes, the Ted Lasso setting) keep you a smooth 25-minute Tube ride away from the action.

- Book refundable if you can. Rates with free cancellation cost a touch more but let you rebook if a better/closer option opens up, or if your plans shift. On an event this size, that flexibility is worth it.

Book now. Thank yourself in November.

### Where to stay: neighborhoods around Olympia

High-level lay of the land so you can pick your zone (Part 3 covers how to move around the city once you're there):

- Right on top of Olympia (West Kensington / W14). The closest hotels are a literal walk to the doors, think [St Paul's Hotel](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=St+Paul%27s+Hotel%2C+153+Hammersmith+Road%2C+London+W14+0QL) (\~0.3 mi), [Residence Inn London Kensington](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Residence+Inn+by+Marriott+London+Kensington%2C+181-183+Warwick+Road%2C+London+W14+8PU) (\~0.4 mi), and [Premier Inn London Hammersmith](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Premier+Inn+London+Hammersmith+Talgarth+Road%2C+181+Talgarth+Road%2C+London+W6+8DN) (\~0.5 mi). Best for: anyone who wants to roll out of bed and be at the venue in five minutes, and drop bags between sessions. Trade-off: books out first, priced at a premium during the event.

- Kensington / Earl's Court. Classic, well-connected, slightly more polished, the kind of postcode that says "I closed a round" without you saying a word. Plenty of options ranging from cute terraced hotels like [Nox](https://www.noxhotels.co.uk/en/hotel-olympia-in-kensington-olympia) and upscale options ([Marriott Kensington](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=London+Marriott+Hotel+Kensington%2C+147c+Cromwell+Road%2C+London+SW5+0TH), boutique spots like [Ember Locke on Cromwell Road](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Ember+Locke%2C+202-220+Cromwell+Road%2C+London+SW5+0SW)) within a mile or so of the venue. Best for: founders, investors, and anyone who wants a nicer base still close to Olympia.

- Hammersmith: The value play. Reliable chains ([Premier Inn on Talgarth Road](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Premier+Inn+London+Hammersmith+Talgarth+Road%2C+181+Talgarth+Road%2C+London+W6+8DN) from \~£45/night off-peak, or the [Shepherds Bush Road location](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Premier+Inn+London+Hammersmith+Shepherds+Bush+Road%2C+229+Shepherds+Bush+Road%2C+London+W6+7AS), [Novotel London West](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Novotel+London+West%2C+1+Shortlands%2C+Hammersmith%2C+London+W6+8DR), [Holiday Inn Express](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Holiday+Inn+Express+London+Hammersmith%2C+120-124+King+Street%2C+London+W6+0QU)) a short hop from the venue with great transit. Best for: solo builders and budget-conscious teams who want the most room for the money.

- Central London (Soho, Shoreditch, King's Cross, etc.). Approximately 25–50 min on the Tube to Olympia while being very close to the nightlife and likely a lot of the side events, plus everything else London has to offer. Best for: people who are prioritizing parties over the main event or want to be in some of London’s “hottest” neighborhoods.

Solo builders and contractors: this is where booking early pays your rent. Every week you wait, flights and hotels climb. A Premier Inn in Hammersmith booked in September beats a premium room booked in October, for less money. Lock it in.

Team leads: block your rooms as a group now. Getting five people into the same hotel near the venue in October is a nightmare; in September it's a phone call. Staying together also makes morning coordination and evening regroups massively easier.

→ Lock in flights and hotels first. Everything else in this article is just details once those two dominoes fall.

## Visas & Travel Docs: Handle This Yesterday

If you're crossing a border to get to London, sort your paperwork first

- Check if you need a visa. The UK government has an official checker, tell it your nationality and purpose and it tells you exactly what you need: [gov.uk/check-uk-visa](http://gov.uk/check-uk-visa). Do this the day you commit, not the week before.
  - Visa support exists. The Solana Foundation has partnered with [Locus](https://breakpoint.golocus.xyz/uk-entry) to help you navigate the visa and ETA process. Note: the Foundation does not cover the cost of visa support; that's on you.

- Passport validity. Make sure your passport has enough validity and blank pages for entry.

Avoid the nightmare scenario: Don't be the person who booked everything early and still couldn't go because the visa lagged. Attendees are responsible for their own entry requirements.

Same theme as Part 1: Breakpoint rewards the prepared and taxes the last-minute. Handle flights, hotels, and visas now, and the rest of this article is just packing a bag.

## Packing for November in London

Here's where your Abu Dhabi or Singapore packing list goes to die. London in mid-November can be a bit chilly, a bit damp, and reliably dark early. Expect daytime highs around 12°C / 54°F, nighttime lows around 5°C / 41°F, roughly 8 hours of daylight, frequent cloud cover, and rain on a meaningful chunk of days. If you show up in shorts and flip-flops (yes, every year someone does), you'll be miserable well before 6pm.

Clothing, the layering system:

- A warm waterproof or water-resistant jacket, this is your MVP. Rain in London comes in quick bursts, so water-resistant beats heavy-but-not-waterproof.

- Layers you can add/remove: tees or base layers, a mid-layer (hoodie/sweater), then the jacket. Conference halls run warm and crowded; the street runs cold and wet. Layering lets you handle both.

- A compact travel umbrella or a jacket with a hood. Locals mostly just deal with it; a foldable umbrella lives in a bag easily.

- Comfortable, broken-in, water-resistant shoes. This is non-negotiable. Olympia is huge, and between the venue, the Tube, and side events, you will walk miles a day. Do not break in new shoes at Breakpoint. Learn from those who have limped through Day 3 before.

- A scarf/beanie/gloves if you run cold, especially for late-night walks between parties.

- Whatever makes you feel like you, there's genuinely no dress code here, from Dunks and hoodies to Patagonia vests to suits if that's your networking getup.

The rest of the bag:

- Universal / UK Type G plug adapter. The UK uses Type G (three rectangular pins) and runs on 230V/50Hz. Your phone and laptop chargers are almost certainly dual-voltage (check the brick for "100–240V"), so you only need a plug adapter, not a voltage converter. Grab one with USB-A + USB-C ports so it doubles as a charging hub. If you forget, most hotels can lend you one and any airport shop sells them (at a premium).

- A high-capacity portable battery. Your single most important piece of gear after your shoes. Between wallet apps, the event app, maps, photos (yes, you're prolly going to take a bunch of selfies while networking), and messaging, your phone will be dead by 2pm. Carry a power bank and top it up nightly. (Note: keep power banks in your carry-on, not checked luggage, airline rule.)

- Charging cables (bring spares, they vanish).

- Business cards or a digital contact method. More on the digital side below, but if you like physical cards, bring plenty. Old-school still works, especially in such a digital world.
  - Pro-Tip: Stickers can double as business cards if done right.

- Refillable water bottle, and any daily meds in your carry-on.

## Tech & Connectivity Setup

Get this dialed before you land so you're not fumbling with settings at Heathrow arrivals.

### Data: get an eSIM if necessary

Don't rely on hotel/venue Wi-Fi, 6,000 people crushing the same network is exactly as bad as it sounds. Check with your telco to see if your plan covers international roaming in the UK, or buy a UK or Europe eSIM before you fly. Modern iPhones and most flagship Androids support eSIM, so you install a data plan digitally and activate it the moment you land, no swapping physical SIMs. Providers like Airalo, Holafly, and others sell UK/Europe plans; pick one with enough data to cover heavy maps + messaging use, and set it to activate on arrival. If you're doing side trips around Europe, buy a regional plan instead of UK-only.

### Your phone: the actual Breakpoint operating system

Set these up in advance:

- The official Breakpoint / event app, download it as soon as it's available. This is your agenda, map, speaker list, and often a useful networking/matchmaking tool. Star the sessions you care about ahead of time (see below).

- Wallet apps loaded and tested (see security section, do this carefully).

- Messaging: Telegram and X are the lifeblood of crypto networking. Make sure your handle is set, your bio says who you are and what you do, you have a PFP for your account, and your DMs are open. A huge amount of "let's meet at Breakpoint" coordination happens in Telegram group chats and X DMs.
  - Pro-Tip: Set your Telegram QR code as your phone lock screen for ease of use.

- A note-taking system you'll actually use, Apple Notes, a voice-memo habit, whatever. You will meet dozens of people and forget 90% of the details by dinner. Capture names, what they're building, and next steps in the moment.

- Maps offline: download the London area in Google/Apple Maps and pin your hotel, Olympia, and side-event venues in advance.

## Security & Wallet Hygiene: Do Not Travel Like a Target

Real talk: Breakpoint is a gathering of thousands of people who visibly work in crypto. That's wonderful for networking and worth being sober about for security. A few non-negotiables:

- Do not travel with your life savings on your phone. Keep the bulk of your holdings in cold storage / a hardware wallet you leave at home, or in accounts you don't need to touch on the trip. Travel with a "hot" wallet holding only what you'd be okay losing, treat it like the cash in your pocket, not your bank vault.

- Hardware wallet, if you must bring one: carry it discreetly, never check it in luggage, and never leave it in a hotel room unsecured.

- Lock everything down: strong device passcode (not a 4-digit), biometric lock, and 2FA on every exchange and email account, using an authenticator app, not SMS..

- Be boring in public. Don't flash balances, don't broadcast your holdings, don't scan random QR codes, and be skeptical of anyone rushing you to "just connect your wallet real quick." A dedicated burner-ish setup (a clean browser profile, a fresh hot wallet, minimal funds) is a pro move

None of this is meant to scare you. It's the same common sense you'd use carrying cash in any big city, just tuned for a crowd where everyone knows what a seed phrase is.

## London Know Before You Go: Personal Security & Wallet Hygiene

London is a major global city, and the same common-sense precautions you would take in New York, Paris, or any other large city apply. Breakpoint also brings together thousands of people who visibly work in crypto, so it is worth being particularly thoughtful about both personal and digital security.

A few important reminders:

- Stay aware of your surroundings. Keep phones, wallets, passports, and bags secure - especially in crowded areas, on public transport, and when entering or leaving venues. Avoid walking while distracted by your phone, and be mindful when using it near roads or station entrances.

- Use trusted transportation. Travel with others where possible, particularly late at night, and use licensed taxis or established ride-hailing services. Do not leave drinks or personal belongings unattended.

- Do not travel with your life savings on your phone. Keep the majority of your holdings in secure cold storage or accounts you will not need during the trip. Use a dedicated travel wallet containing only what you would be comfortable losing and treat it like the cash in your pocket, not your bank vault.

- If you must bring a hardware wallet, keep it discreet. Carry it with you rather than placing it in checked luggage, and never leave it unsecured in a hotel room or shared space.

- Secure your devices and accounts. Use a strong passcode, biometric protection, device tracking, and two-factor authentication through an authenticator app rather than SMS. Install software updates before traveling and avoid accessing sensitive accounts over unsecured public Wi-Fi.

- Be discreet in public. Do not display wallet balances, discuss personal holdings with strangers, or advertise where assets are stored. Never share a seed phrase or recovery code - no legitimate person, application, or Breakpoint team member will ask for one.

- Treat unexpected links and QR codes with caution. Verify the source before connecting a wallet, signing a transaction, downloading an application, or scanning a code. Be especially skeptical of anyone creating urgency or asking you to “connect your wallet quickly.”

- Consider a clean travel setup. A separate browser profile, fresh hot wallet, limited funds, and only the applications you need can significantly reduce your exposure.

This guidance is not intended to cause concern. It is the same practical awareness you should use when carrying cash or valuable devices in any major city, adapted for an event where digital assets are part of the conversation.

## Pre-Load Your Schedule (So You're Not Doom-Scrolling at 2am)

The rookie move is showing up Day 1 and then figuring out where to be. By then the best side events are full, so o it now instead:

- Map the whole week, not just the main days. Remember from Part 1: Hacker House (Nov 1–12), 200ms (Nov 13), Scale or Die (Nov 14), and BlockZero (Nov 15) all orbit the main event, plus dozens of community side events. Decide which you're attending and register early, several are application-only or capacity-capped.

- Browse and RSVP to side events via the official community list: [luma.com/BP-SideEvents](http://luma.com/BP-SideEvents). The best mixers and dinners fill up before the conference even starts..

- Star your must-see sessions in the event app, then build the rest of your day around the people you want to meet

- Tie it back to your goal. Use it as the filter: if a session, mixer, or party doesn't move you toward that goal, it's optional. This is how you avoid FOMO-driven chaos and actually get what you came for.

- Leave white space. Do not schedule every hour. The best Breakpoint moments, the hallway conversation, the impromptu coffee, the "come to this dinner" invite, happen in the gaps. Over-scheduling kills serendipity.
