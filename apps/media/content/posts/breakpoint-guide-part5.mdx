_Part 5/5 in the[ @SolanaEvents](https://x.com/@SolanaEvents) guide to thriving (and surviving) at Breakpoint London 2026_

Olympia is packed up, your feet might file a formal complaint, and your camera roll is 40% selfies with people whose handles you're praying you wrote down. Breakpoint is over. Now it pays off: in the weeks after, when a good conversation turns into a call, a collab, a hire, or a friendship. Don't let that momentum die in DMs.

The conference ends. The conversations can keep going.

## The Week After Breakpoint: Sort the Pile

You're home, Olympia is behind you, and the week after Breakpoint is here. Start by going through everything you collected (contacts, selfies, notes, business cards, stickers, Telegram chats, that QR you scanned at 1am) and sort it into three tiers:

- Hot: People you made a specific plan with, who tie directly to your goal, or who asked you to follow up. These come first.
- Warm: A great conversation with a possible next step. These come next.
- Good to know: Fun chat, no obvious next step. Follow them, engage with their posts, no DM required.

For hot and warm contacts, jot down where you met, what you talked about, and what you promised. This is where the "with context" part of Article 4 pays off.

## Keep Your Promises First

Said you'd send a link, a deck, or an intro? Do it before you ask for anything. It earns the second conversation and gives you a natural reason to reach out.

> "hey! as promised, here's the onboarding doc we talked about at the payments booth. no rush, curious what you think. - maurice (@mauricedotxyz)"

The easiest follow-up to send is the one you already promised.

## This Is the Week to Follow Up

A week out is the sweet spot. People have had time to get home and dig out of the backlog, and the conversation is still fresh. This is the week to send your follow-ups, before you turn into "who?"

Keep it short and specific:

- Where you met: "We chatted at the Hacker House demo night."
- What you talked about: The specific thing, not "crypto."
- One clear ask: Feedback, an intro, a resource.
- An easy out: Make "not right now" comfortable.

> "hey, maurice (@mauricedotxyz) here, we talked stablecoin payouts by the main stage on day 2. you mentioned your team is testing new payment rails. would you be open to trading notes async? totally fine if the timing's off."

Not this:

> "gm ser. great to connect. let's hop on a call?"

Match the channel and don't spam. Met on X? Follow up on X. Swapped Telegram? Use Telegram. One thoughtful message beats a copy-paste blast across four platforms. And please don't add people to group chats without asking. Nobody wants to wake up in "BP London Alpha Chat" with 47 strangers.

## Give Them Time

No reply? Don't take it personally, and definitely don't triple text. Plenty of people travel after Breakpoint, head straight to another event, or come home to a launch, a mountain of work, or a family that missed them. Silence usually means busy.

The one-nudge rule: After a week or two, send one friendly bump:

> "bumping this in case it got buried post-breakpoint. no worries if now's not the time!"

Still nothing? Let it go, keep engaging with their posts, and the door stays open. Some of the best collabs start with a reply months later.

## Async First, Calls Later

Five great minutes at a booth doesn't mean someone wants a call. People are busy, spread across time zones, and protective of their calendars. Start async with a link, a doc, a short Loom, or a question.

Save the call for when there's a real reason: a deeper collaboration, getting hired, joining a team, or founding something together. When you get there, share a calendar link (Calendly, Cal.com, whatever you use) and skip the scheduling ping-pong.

> "this feels like it'd be easier to talk through live. here's my cal link if you're up for 20 min sometime next week: \[link\]"

Making an intro? Double opt-in: ask both sides first, then give context so they know why they're talking.

## Recap It, Codify It, Share It

Don't let a week of ideas, demos, and alpha evaporate.

- Write it down. Notes, a private doc, or journaling all count. What surprised you? Which teams are you watching now?
- Post your recap. Tag the people, projects, and talks that stood out, and share takeaways, not just photos. "Three things from Breakpoint London that changed how I think about agentic payments" beats a carousel of lanyards. A thread, an article, or a first-person video all work.
- Catch what you missed. Talks are posted to X and YouTube after the event. Replays make great openers: "caught your talk on the replay, had a question about how you're handling \[fill in the blank\]."
- Build in public. Came through Hacker House or pitched on the floor? Keep shipping, post progress, and tag the people who gave feedback.

If you learned something, share it. Someone else in the ecosystem probably needs it.

## Stay Secure (The Scammers Went to Breakpoint Too)

Thousands of people just met hundreds of strangers, and bad actors know it.

- Fake "we met at Breakpoint" DMs. Don't remember them and they're pushing a link or "quick opportunity"? Be skeptical.
- Impersonators. Check the handle, followers, and mutuals before replying.
- Fake POAPs, airdrops, and claim links. Never connect your wallet to something you didn't seek out.
- "Just download this app for our call." Hard no.

## Recover + Handle the Admin

- Jet lag hits on the way home too. Hydrate, get sunlight, and don't book your biggest call the morning after you land.
- The post-con blues are real. Channel them into your follow-ups and your recap.
- File receipts and expenses before they vanish into your inbox.

## Measure Your ROI

Pull the Here for / Lead with / Ask for table from Article 4 back up and be honest. Hiring conversations, collaborators, client calls, investor interest: what actually turned into a next step?

Then check in again at 30, 60, and 90 days. Breakpoint ROI shows up when a DM from November turns into a contract in February, or the person from the coffee queue becomes a co-founder.

## Your Post-Breakpoint Timeline

- This week: Sort your contacts, keep your promises, and follow up with your hot and warm contacts. Write your notes while they're fresh.
- Next week: Post your recap.
- Weeks 3-4: Move the right conversations to calls. Make your intros.
- Day 30, 60, 90: Check your ROI. Stay plugged in through Discords, local meetups, Superteam chapters, hackathons, and @SolanaEvents.
