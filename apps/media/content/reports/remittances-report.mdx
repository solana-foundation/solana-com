Money transfer operators can no longer treat a remittance as a transaction that ends at pickup.&#x20;

Sending $200 across a border still costs 6.49% and clears in three to five days, while hundreds of billions sit idle in pre-funded nostro accounts. From payroll corridors and gig-worker payouts to family transfers into India, Mexico, and the Philippines, every flow is a chance to open a wallet, a savings product, or a credit history.

A new model is taking its place, built on high-speed rails, plug-and-play composable infrastructure, and emerging regulation: remittances that settle nearly instantly, cost a fraction of a cent to move, reach people who have a smartphone but no bank, and turn a one-time transfer into an ongoing pipeline of services.
