# Remove Inactive Stakes From Rewards

Q4 2026 • Solana Foundation

Partitioned epoch rewards currently consider every stake account on the network,
including ones with no effective and no activating stake. Those accounts earn
nothing — there is no stake to reward. But their presence is not free: the set
of accounts under consideration determines **how many partitions** the rewards
period is split into and feeds **the lattice hash**, and both are
consensus-critical. Every validator implementation therefore has to include
accounts that cannot possibly receive anything, and has to include them the same
way, or the cluster forks.

[SIMD-0599](https://github.com/solana-foundation/solana-improvement-documents/blob/main/proposals/0599-rm-inactive-stakes-from-per.md)
removes them. Only activating, active, and deactivating stakes take part in the
calculation and distribution phases.

No staker receives more or less than before. This is a change to which accounts
the protocol has to carry through an expensive, consensus-critical calculation,
not to who gets paid.

## Technical Details

### What "Inactive" Means Here

An inactive stake is one with **no effective and no activating stake**. It is
worth being precise about the boundary, because a deactivating stake still has
effective stake and is therefore not inactive — it is still earning, and it is
still included.

### The Rule

During the rollover from epoch `E` to `E + 1`, stakes that were inactive in `E`
are excluded from partitioned epoch rewards — both the calculation phase and
the distribution phase. Separately, during account updates, a stake account
inactive in **both** the current and previous epochs is dropped from
consideration for a validator's stake weight.

The full matrix:

| Status in E         | Status in E+1       | During rewards for E |
| ------------------- | ------------------- | -------------------- |
| Activating / active | Activating / active | Include              |
| Active              | Inactive            | **Include**          |
| Inactive            | Inactive            | Exclude              |

### The Edge Case That Decides the Design

Row two is the one that matters. A stake with non-zero effective stake in `E`
that becomes inactive at the start of `E + 1` **must still be included** and must
still receive its rewards during the payout.

It earned those rewards in `E`. The fact that it went inactive before the payout
happened does not un-earn them, and dropping it because of its status at payout
time would silently underpay a staker who did nothing wrong. This is why the
rule keys on status in the reward epoch rather than status at distribution time.

Note also that during the calculation and distribution phases of partitioned
epoch rewards, stake state changes are not permitted through transactions — the
status cannot shift underneath the calculation once it is running.

### What Changes Observably

Nothing in anyone's balance. What changes is the partition count: with fewer
accounts in the reward set, the rewards period is divided into fewer partitions,
so a distribution period may be shorter than an equivalent one before
activation. Anything that tracks the length of the rewards period, or expects a
particular number of partitions for a given epoch, is reading a number that
moves at activation.

The lattice hash also changes as a consequence, which is exactly why this needs
a feature gate rather than being a client-side optimization.

### Affected Validator Components

The proposal lists one:

| Validator Component             | Impact                            |
| ------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Transaction Execution (Runtime) | Partitioned epoch rewards changed |

Conformance will be established with a ledger containing active, inactive, and
deactivating stakes at epoch `E`, covering every row of the table above.

## About This Upgrade

This is a maintenance change, and the case for it is stated plainly in the
proposal: including inactive stakes is "useless, but consensus critical." That
combination is the worst one available. Work that produces no result is merely
wasteful; work that produces no result and must be replicated bit-for-bit across
every independent validator implementation is a standing source of divergence
bugs for no benefit at all.

Removing it makes the rewards period cheaper, makes validator implementations
simpler, and removes a footgun that every new client has to rediscover. The
guard rail is the edge case in row two of the table: a stake that earned in the
reward epoch gets paid, whatever happened to it since.
