# Block Revenue Sharing

Q4 2026 • Solana Foundation

Delegated stake decides how many blocks a validator produces. More stake means
more slots in the leader schedule, which means more block revenue — the fees
collected from the transactions in those blocks. None of that revenue has ever
reached the delegators whose stake generated it, because the protocol had no
mechanism to divert it. Inflation rewards are shared; block revenue is not.

[SIMD-0123](https://github.com/solana-foundation/solana-improvement-documents/blob/main/proposals/0123-block-revenue-distribution.md)
adds that mechanism. A validator sets a **block revenue commission** in its vote
account; the protocol takes that share for the validator and routes the rest to
its delegators, paid out at the next epoch boundary through the same partitioned
rewards machinery that already distributes inflation rewards.

This is opt-in and the default keeps today's behavior. A vote account with no
block revenue commission and no collector set collects everything into the
validator's identity account, exactly as now.

## Migration Checklist

<AudienceGroup>
<Audience title="For Users and Stakers" summary="Possibly more yield, but the rewards arrive unstaked.">

If you delegate stake, you may start earning a share of the block revenue your
validator produces — but only from validators that choose to share it. Ask your
validator what block revenue commission they have set.

One thing to know: these rewards arrive as **inactive SOL** in your stake
account. Unlike inflation rewards, they are not automatically added to your
active delegation. You have to restake them yourself if you want them earning.

</Audience>

<Audience title="For Validator Operators" summary="Opt in by setting a block revenue commission. Nothing changes if you don't.">

Sharing is opt-in and requires vote account state version 4.

- Update your vote account to v4
  ([SIMD-0185](https://github.com/solana-foundation/solana-improvement-documents/blob/main/proposals/0185-vote-account-v4.md)).
  Until you do, all block revenue keeps going to your identity account.
- Set a block revenue commission with `UpdateCommissionBps`, using the new
  `CommissionKind::BlockRevenue` kind. The rate is in basis points and is capped
  at 10,000 (100%) when applied.
- Set a commission collector address
  ([SIMD-0232](https://github.com/solana-foundation/solana-improvement-documents/blob/main/proposals/0232-custom-commission-collector.md)).
  **If the collector is not system-owned, or would not be rent-exempt after the
  deposit, your commission is burned** — not redirected, burned.
- Know that the rate is read from the vote account state as of the beginning of
  the previous epoch, the same state used to build the current leader schedule.
  A change takes effect an epoch later, not immediately.
- Do not plan to close a vote account that has pending delegator rewards
  ([details](#breaking-change-vote-accounts-with-pending-rewards-cannot-close)).

</Audience>

<Audience title="For Indexers and Analytics" summary="Revenue attribution and reward accounting both change.">

**If you attribute block revenue to validators**
([details](#breaking-change-block-revenue-has-new-destinations)):

- Stop assuming block revenue lands in the block producer's identity account.
  It may split between a commission collector account and the producer's vote
  account.
- Resolve the producer through its vote account
  ([SIMD-0180](https://github.com/solana-foundation/solana-improvement-documents/blob/main/proposals/0180-vote-account-leader-schedule.md)),
  then read the commission rate and collector from vote state v4.

**If you report rewards or staking yield**
([details](#breaking-change-a-second-reward-stream)):

- Expect a second stream of rewards into stake accounts at epoch boundaries,
  distinct from inflation rewards.
- Do not read it from the epoch rewards sysvar's `total_rewards` or
  `distributed_rewards` fields. Block revenue deliberately does not touch them —
  it is tracked through the sysvar's **lamport balance** instead.
- Expect a stake account's delegation and its balance to diverge, because block
  rewards land as inactive lamports.

</Audience>

</AudienceGroup>

## Breaking Changes in Detail

<AudienceGroup>
<Audience title="For Anyone Attributing Revenue" summary="Revenue can now land in three places instead of one.">

### Breaking Change: Block Revenue Has New Destinations

Today all block revenue for a block goes to the producing validator's identity
account. After activation the runtime looks up the block producer's **vote
account** and checks whether it declares a block revenue commission rate and a
collector address.

If it does not — including because the vote account has not been upgraded to
state version 4 — everything goes to the identity account exactly as before.
This is the path most validators will be on at activation, which is what makes
the change easy to miss.

If it does, the revenue splits:

- The **commission** goes to the commission collector account, but only if that
  account is system-program owned and remains rent-exempt after the deposit.
  Otherwise the commission is **burned**.
- The **remainder** is added to the vote account's `pending_delegator_rewards`
  field and to its lamport balance, but only if the vote account is vote-program
  owned and initialized at state version 4 or later. Otherwise it too is
  **burned**.

A pipeline that sums identity account credits to measure validator revenue does
not error after this change. It reports a number that gets quietly smaller as
validators opt in, and reads that as declining validator income rather than as
revenue moving somewhere it is not looking.

</Audience>

<Audience title="For Rewards Reporting" summary="Block rewards are a separate stream with separate accounting.">

### Breaking Change: A Second Reward Stream

Block revenue is accumulated per block but distributed per epoch. At the
beginning of each epoch the pending delegator rewards are swept out of actively
staked vote accounts into the epoch rewards sysvar and then paid to stake
accounts through the partitioned distribution mechanism from
[SIMD-0118](https://github.com/solana-foundation/solana-improvement-documents/blob/main/proposals/0118-partitioned-epoch-reward-distribution.md).

That reuses the inflation rewards pipeline but deliberately **not** its
accounting. Block revenue does not update the epoch rewards sysvar's
`total_rewards` or `distributed_rewards` fields. It is tracked entirely through
the sysvar's lamport balance, which is debited as each partition pays out.

The consequence for anything reading rewards: the sysvar's internal counters
describe inflation rewards only. Reporting total staker income from those fields
will now undercount, without erroring, by exactly the amount of block revenue
being shared.

Rewards also behave differently once they arrive. Inflation rewards are added to
a stake account's active delegation. Block revenue rewards are not — they land
as inactive lamports the staker has to restake. A stake account's balance and
its delegation will diverge, and the delegation calculation accounts for this
explicitly:

```text
post_delegation = min(
    delegation + stake_rewards,
    balance + stake_rewards + block_rewards - rent_exempt_reserve
)
```

This was a deliberate trade. Automatically activating the rewards would have
required extra protocol complexity;
[SIMD-0022](https://github.com/solana-foundation/solana-improvement-documents/blob/main/proposals/0022-multi-stake.md)
aims to improve the experience separately by letting stake accounts delegate an
unstaked balance.

</Audience>

<Audience title="For Validator Tooling" summary="A vote account holding pending rewards is not closeable.">

### Breaking Change: Vote Accounts With Pending Rewards Cannot Close

Pending delegator rewards sit in the vote account's lamport balance between the
block that earned them and the epoch boundary that distributes them. Those
lamports are owed to delegators, so they must not be withdrawable.

The `Withdraw` instruction is changed accordingly: while
`pending_delegator_rewards` is non-zero, the withdrawable balance is the vote
account's balance minus `pending_delegator_rewards` minus the minimum
rent-exempt balance. The vote account **cannot be closed by fully withdrawing**
during that window.

Any automation that closes vote accounts — migration scripts, decommissioning
tooling — needs to handle a withdrawal that cannot drain the account. The window
closes at the next epoch boundary, when the pending field resets to zero.

</Audience>

</AudienceGroup>

## Technical Details

### Collection, Per Block

After all transactions in a block are processed, the runtime resolves the block
producer's vote account
([SIMD-0180](https://github.com/solana-foundation/solana-improvement-documents/blob/main/proposals/0180-vote-account-leader-schedule.md))
and reads its block revenue commission rate and collector address. Both are read
from the vote account state **as of the beginning of the previous epoch** — the
same snapshot used to build the current epoch's leader schedule
([SIMD-0232](https://github.com/solana-foundation/solana-improvement-documents/blob/main/proposals/0232-custom-commission-collector.md)).
A commission change therefore takes effect an epoch after it is made.

The commission is the revenue multiplied by the lesser of the declared rate and
10,000 basis points, divided by 10,000 with integer division and the remainder
discarded. The delegator share is whatever is left.

### Distribution, Per Epoch

Delegator rewards are calculated at the first block of epoch `N` — defined as
any block whose parent is in the previous epoch — and recalculated if a node
restarts during the distribution period.

For each vote account, let `A` be its total active stake delegation during epoch
`N - 1` and `P` its `pending_delegator_rewards` at the end of `N - 1`:

- If `P` is zero, the vote account is skipped.
- `pending_delegator_rewards` is reset to `0` at the first block of `N`.
- If `A` is zero, the reset alone effectively returns the rewards to the voter.
- Otherwise `P` lamports are debited from the vote account and credited to the
  epoch rewards sysvar, before any transactions or votes are processed.

Each stake account's share is its active stake in `N - 1` multiplied by `P`,
divided by `A` with integer division, truncating any sub-lamport remainder.
Those amounts are partitioned and paid out per SIMD-0118, with the sysvar's
lamport balance debited at each slot. When distribution completes, the sysvar
balance is reset to its rent exemption and any surplus is burned.

### Vote Program Changes

**`UpdateCommissionBps`**, added in
[SIMD-0291](https://github.com/solana-foundation/solana-improvement-documents/blob/main/proposals/0291-commission-rate-in-basis-points.md),
gains support for `CommissionKind::BlockRevenue`, which previously returned
`InstructionError::InvalidInstructionData`. The stored rate may be any `u16`,
but is capped at 10,000 when the commission is actually calculated.

**`DepositDelegatorRewards`** is new, with discriminant `19u32`:

```rust
pub enum VoteInstruction {
    /// # Account references
    ///   0. `[WRITE]` Vote account to be updated with the deposit
    ///   1. `[SIGNER, WRITE]` Source account for deposit funds
    DepositDelegatorRewards { // 19u32
        deposit: u64,
    },
}
```

It returns `InstructionError::NotEnoughAccountKeys` with fewer than two
accounts, and `InstructionError::InvalidAccountData` if the vote account fails
to deserialize or is not at state version 4. On success it performs a system
transfer CPI of `deposit` lamports into the vote account and increments
`pending_delegator_rewards`.

### Dependencies

SIMD-0123 builds on five accepted proposals, and none of this works without
them: SIMD-0180 (vote account keys the leader schedule), SIMD-0185 (vote account
v4, which holds the new fields), SIMD-0232 (custom commission collector),
SIMD-0291 (commission rates in basis points), and
[SIMD-0392](https://github.com/solana-foundation/solana-improvement-documents/blob/main/proposals/0392-rent-increase-adaptations.md)
(the delegation calculation this adapts).

### What It Replaces

Validators have built their own revenue sharing for years, because the protocol
offered none. Those arrangements work, but they rest on the operator's
compliance rather than on consensus: Jito's tip distribution program instructs
operators to divert tips and relies on auditing to catch those who do not, and
the Cogent validator diverts fee revenue to NFT holders under terms the holders
have to police themselves. Both also require trusting a third party's reward
calculation, and Jito's distributes through a merkle tree without restaking the
result.

Doing it in the protocol removes the trust requirement. The commission rate is
onchain, the split is computed by every validator, and a rate that is set is a
rate that is enforced.

## About This Upgrade

Block revenue sharing closes a gap that has been visible since fees became a
meaningful share of validator income. Stake earns its delegator a share of
inflation but nothing of the fees that the stake's own leader slots generate,
and the only fixes available were out-of-protocol and trust-based.

The design is conservative about defaults. Nothing changes for a validator that
does not act, the commission cap is 100%, and revenue from validators still on
vote state v3 flows exactly where it flows today. What changes immediately is
the accounting: block revenue gains two new possible destinations and stakers
gain a second reward stream that is tracked separately from the first, which
makes this a breaking change for anyone measuring either.
