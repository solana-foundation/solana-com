# Agave 4.4 Release Overview

**Updated October 2026** • Solana Foundation

Agave 4.4 is the first release after the consensus switch.
[Alpenglow](/upgrades/alpenglow) activates on mainnet under
[Agave 4.3](/upgrades/agave-4-3-release-overview), at epoch 1058, so by the time
4.4 begins activating features the network is already finalizing blocks in
roughly 150ms.

The **first** feature to activate in 4.4 is
**[fast leader handover](/upgrades/fast-leader-handover)**, Alpenglow's next
step. It lets an incoming leader start building before the network has
confirmed its parent, and it can only take effect on a cluster already running
Alpenglow. After that comes the largest set of runtime changes in years: a
constant-cost program entrypoint, direct mapping of account data, two new
cryptographic syscalls, the end of sBPF v0-v2 deployments, and protocol-level
sharing of block revenue with stakers. Each activates on its own feature gate.

Anza's
[v4.4 release schedule](https://github.com/anza-xyz/agave/wiki/v4.4-Release-Schedule)
puts testnet adoption at September 28, 2026, devnet at October 5, and mainnet
general adoption at **November 2**, with feature activation beginning **November
9**. The schedule is explicitly tentative and every date on it is subject to
change.

## Technical Details

### Fast Leader Handover Activates First

Under Alpenglow, a leader waits for a **parent-ready** signal — the consensus
pool deciding that a block is an acceptable parent for its slot — before it
produces. That wait is dead time at every handover between leaders.
[Fast leader handover](/upgrades/fast-leader-handover) removes it: the leader
starts building on the parent it expects to be ready, and switches parents
mid-block if the network resolves differently.

It is specified in
[SIMD-0326](https://github.com/solana-foundation/solana-improvement-documents/blob/main/proposals/0326-alpenglow.md),
section 2.7 of the Alpenglow white paper, and has its own gate,
`FLHoAWBDjNh6zwmJ5i1NKK4KyD8otAiv7XxvmnFnVnKH`, separate from the `alpenglow`
consensus gate. The wire encoding for the parent switch — the `BlockHeader` and
`UpdateParent` block markers — is specified separately in
[SIMD-0337](https://github.com/solana-foundation/solana-improvement-documents/blob/main/proposals/0337-parent-ready-update-marker.md).

<FeatureActivationStatus featureAddress="FLHoAWBDjNh6zwmJ5i1NKK4KyD8otAiv7XxvmnFnVnKH" />

**This is the 4.4 change streaming consumers must be ready for.** Switching
parents mid-block produces a new bank, and to Geyser a new bank is a new block.
Before this, a slot carried two banks only when a leader equivocated; after it,
Anza expects a second bank in under 1% of slots. Rare, but frequent enough that
a pipeline keyed on slot alone will eventually fuse two candidate blocks into
one. The fix is the `bank_id` migration that ships with Alpenglow in 4.3: buffer
per `(slot, bank_id)` and promote the bank that reaches `Confirmed`
([details](/upgrades/alpenglow#breaking-change-one-slot-can-contain-more-than-one-bank)).
Adopt it before this gate activates.

### Program Performance

Three changes make programs cheaper to run, and they compound.

**[Direct account pointers in program input](/upgrades/direct-account-pointers)**
([SIMD-0449](https://github.com/solana-foundation/solana-improvement-documents/blob/main/proposals/0449-direct-account-pointers-in-program-input.md))
serializes a pointer per instruction account into the program input, so an
entrypoint no longer walks the accounts section to find its boundaries. The
entrypoint cost stops scaling with account count: a 64-account instruction goes
from 504 CUs to 11.

**[Account data direct mapping](/upgrades/account-data-direct-mapping)** maps
account data into the VM address space instead of copying it in and back out
again. No SIMD — it is an implementation change behind feature gate
`CR3dVN2Yoo95Y96kLSTaziWDAQT2MNEpiWh5cqVq2pNE`.

**[Virtual address space adjustments](/upgrades/virtual-address-space)**
([SIMD-0460](https://github.com/solana-foundation/solana-improvement-documents/blob/main/proposals/0460-virtual-address-space-adjustments.md))
is the half of SIMD-0219 covering address translation and memory mapping. It
also deactivates stack frame gaps globally, which compacts the stack address
space. Programs that depend on the old stack layout — absolute addressing,
addressing across stack frames, or a bug that read past a frame boundary — are
the ones at risk.

### New Cryptography

Two syscalls expose primitives the validator already links.

**[SHA-512 syscall](/upgrades/sha512-syscall)**
([SIMD-0512](https://github.com/solana-foundation/solana-improvement-documents/blob/main/proposals/0512-sha512-syscall.md))
adds `sol_sha512` with the same interface as `sol_sha256`. A SHA-512 hash that
costs thousands of CUs in sBPF costs under 100 through the syscall.

**[Big integer ModExp syscall](/upgrades/modexp-syscall)**
([SIMD-0529](https://github.com/solana-foundation/solana-improvement-documents/blob/main/proposals/0529-big-integer-modexp-syscall.md))
adds `sol_big_mod_exp`, computing `(base ^ exponent) mod modulus` on operands up
to 512 bytes — enough for 4096-bit RSA. It is the analogue of Ethereum's EIP-198
precompile, with a Solana syscall interface rather than the EVM ABI.

Both join the curve syscalls added in Agave 4.0
([new cryptography schemes](/upgrades/new-cryptography)).

### Program Deployment

**[sBPFv3 programs](/upgrades/sbpfv3-programs)**
([SIMD-0500](https://github.com/solana-foundation/solana-improvement-documents/blob/main/proposals/0500-disable-deployment-of-sbpf-v0-v1-v2.md))
makes sBPFv3 the minimum target for new deployments, redeployments, and
finalization. Programs already onchain keep running.

**[Loader v3: program data sized to the ELF](/upgrades/loader-v3-elf-length)**
([SIMD-0433](https://github.com/solana-foundation/solana-improvement-documents/blob/main/proposals/0433-loader-v3-set-program-data-to-elf-length.md))
makes `Upgrade` resize the program data account to match the ELF being deployed,
in both directions. Shrinking refunds the surplus rent to the spill account;
growing no longer needs a separate `ExtendProgram` first. Anyone deliberately
over-provisioning program data space loses that space on the next upgrade.

Agave 4.4 also fixes program cache deployment verification. There is no SIMD and
no feature gate — it is a client fix, not a protocol change.

### Economics

**[Block revenue sharing](/upgrades/block-revenue-sharing)**
([SIMD-0123](https://github.com/solana-foundation/solana-improvement-documents/blob/main/proposals/0123-block-revenue-distribution.md))
lets a validator share block revenue with its delegators through the protocol
rather than through a side arrangement. A vote account sets a block revenue
commission in basis points; the remainder accrues to `pending_delegator_rewards`
and is paid out at the next epoch boundary through the existing partitioned
rewards mechanism. Validators that never set a commission keep collecting
everything into their identity account, as today.

**[Remove inactive stakes from rewards](/upgrades/inactive-stakes-rewards)**
([SIMD-0599](https://github.com/solana-foundation/solana-improvement-documents/blob/main/proposals/0599-rm-inactive-stakes-from-per.md))
stops including stake accounts with no effective or activating stake in
partitioned epoch rewards. They never earned anything; they were
consensus-critical anyway, because they moved the partition count and the
lattice hash.

**[Reduced rent](/upgrades/reduced-rent)**
([SIMD-0437](https://github.com/solana-foundation/solana-improvement-documents/blob/main/proposals/0437-incremental-rent-reduction.md))
continues here with steps 3 through 5 of the five-step cut to
`lamports_per_byte`. Steps 1 and 2 activated in September 2026 under Agave 4.2.

## About This Upgrade

Agave 4.4 is two things, and they are worth keeping separate in your planning.

The first is fast leader handover, which activates first. It is the
consensus-side follow-up to Alpenglow, invisible to anyone sending transactions
and consequential for anyone reassembling blocks from a stream: it is what turns
more than one bank per slot from an equivocation artifact into a routine event.
The `bank_id` migration it depends on is already available in 4.3.

The second is a runtime release. Program developers get a materially cheaper
entrypoint and two cryptographic primitives that were previously impractical,
and in exchange have to deploy sBPFv3 and stop relying on the old stack address
layout. Validators and stakers get the first protocol-level mechanism for
sharing block revenue.

Every change activates on its own feature gate. Track them individually rather
than treating "4.4" as a single switch, and read Anza's schedule for current
dates — every one of them is tentative.
